11 November 2026 · Investigation · What was opened

There is no 2026 Federal Court case in the records that were opened

The question was whether the Federal Court of Australia had a case, in 2026, about Dr Richard William McLean, also known as Barran Dodger. The public repository list was searched. The files whose names say Federal Court, FCA, or court and 2026 were opened. No file number, no originating application, no judgment, and no order of the Federal Court dated 2026 was in them.

The 137-page examination, The Divine Contrast, speaks of the Federal Court only through the 27 March 2023 public-interest-disclosure assessment. It does not identify a 2026 proceeding.

What people are calling a Federal Court determination

The document is this email: Formal Notification of Carer Appointment, Federal Court Determination, and Revocation of Guardianship Powers. The message header is from Rich McLean, richarddrawsstuff@gmail.com, on Wednesday 12 November at 10:46 pm, to kathleendehar@gmail.com and others. A note later in the same file dates the document 12 November 2025. Wednesday 12 November 2025 matches that header. It is not a 2026 filing.

The body is written as if Kathleen Dehar is the appointed carer, writing to Philip Glass at the NSW Trustee and Guardian. It says Scott Treadwell has confirmed employment with the Department of Social Services, that this confirmation is a Federal Court ruling, that it overrides the Administrative Appeals Tribunal and Comcare, that it revokes guardianship and the authority of NCAT and Sukhi Tear immediately, and that it creates an entitlement to workers’ compensation, back pay and interest under section 19 of the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988. It cites section 75(v) of the Constitution. Those are claims in the email. They are not orders.

The letter the email relies on was opened. It is Scott Tredwell, General Counsel, 27 March 2023. The same letter is also saved under the name Federal court .pdf. It is an authorised officer’s decision under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013. He records that he is satisfied, on the information given, of Department of Social Services employment, and that he is prepared to assume the conduct is disclosable. He then decides not to allocate the disclosure, because he is not satisfied it was made to an authorised recipient of conduct relating to the Federal Court. He says no further action will be taken on that material. He does not order compensation. He does not revoke a guardianship or financial-management order. He does not sit as a judge giving judgment. A decision not to allocate a disclosure is not a ruling that employment is binding on every other agency, and it is not a ruling that NCAT’s order of 16 October 2024 has ceased.

Section 75(v) of the Constitution gives the High Court power to hear certain cases against officers of the Commonwealth. It does not turn a letter from the Court’s general counsel into a judgment, and it does not let a private email revoke a tribunal order.
