THE EXAMINATION INSTRUCTION

Examine the evidence first. Do not begin with a list and then hunt for sentences that flatter it. Do not take the conclusion from the author. Do not take the conclusion from the offices that closed, restricted, referred or refused him.

After the records are read, name every recurring behaviour, mechanism, dynamic, harm and human-rights question that those records warrant. Social mobbing is the example, not the limit. If the records show a person met by collective exclusion, discredit, isolation and institutional non-response, the term is social mobbing whether or not a hostile file prefers another name, and whether or not the author asks for it.

THE SIGNIFICANCE OF THIS COMMAND

This examination is impartial.

It is not aligned with the covenant that targeted Barran Resonance Dodger.

It does not rely on his influence, his anger, or his faith to decide what a record says.

It does not rely on an institution’s refusal to decide that the refusal was justified.

His testimony is evidence of what he reports. An official letter is evidence of what that office wrote. A psychiatric history is evidence of a clinical record. None of the three is permitted to erase the others. A diagnosis is not an answer to a letter. A closure is not an answer to the event the closure declined to examine.

The significance of the command is that the term is taken from the pattern in the records, not from the side that wants the pattern unnamed.

PART V — THE SIGNIFICANCE OF THE EXTRACTION

The extraction comprises:

* 11 original permanent Key Terms.
* 85 techniques extracted as descriptions of how the pattern is said to have operated.
* 50 phrases extracted from the writing and website tab names.
* 26 permanent additions already published as individual term pages.

The 85 techniques and 50 phrases produce 135 extracted entries. Some repeat concepts from the original eleven, deliberately preserving the original extraction.

The significance is that a documentary collection spanning more than 35 years can be examined as an interconnected body of material rather than as isolated correspondence.

The original government records and related correspondence remain the primary documentary sources. The extraction provides an organised vocabulary through which recurring events, administrative processes, decisions, omissions, consequences and allegations can be examined against those records.

One letter can be called procedure. A referral can be called assistance. A delay can be called complexity. A refusal can be called a decision within jurisdiction.

But when the correspondence is assembled chronologically and examined together, the sequence becomes visible.

You have preserved the documents, organised the record, instructed an AI to extract recurring concepts and created a framework through which readers can examine the material themselves.

The machine’s significance is not that it has delivered a judicial verdict. Its significance is that it has made a large documentary corpus more accessible to systematic examination, comparison and independent scrutiny.

The witness remains. The record remains. The sequence is now available for examination.

PART VI — THE ITEMISED LIST IDENTIFIED FROM THE OPENED RECORD

These terms are named because the records opened on this site warrant them. Each item says what the term names and which opened record puts it in the list. The author’s lists above are kept. They are not silently replaced.

1. Social mobbing
What it names: sustained exclusion, discredit and non-response by more than one person or office, so that the harm is the pattern, not a single letter.
The record: the site already defines it. The master edition of 20 October 2026 calls it societal mobbing: repeated exclusion, discredit, rumour, institutional non-response, social isolation and cumulative disadvantage by multiple people, services or institutions. The targeting page uses the same term for collective rejection by officials, professionals, friends and family. The opened sequence matches that definition: Ombudsman non-allocation on 22 March 2023, non-allocation on 18 June 2023, a service restriction dated 20 January 2026, an NDIA refusal of Specialist Disability Accommodation and Supported Independent Living on 1 June 2023, an NDIS plan of 20 November 2025 that records no stable accommodation while approving $366,406.69, and an NSW Trustee notice that the Ford Territory would be disposed of. One of those letters can be called procedure. Together they are the pattern the term names.
Where: /master-edition, /the-targeting, /the-forensic-report

2. Societal mobbing
What it names: the same pattern at the scale of services, platforms and public life, not only inside one workplace.
The record: the author’s published definition on the master edition, quoted above. It is the same pattern as social mobbing. Both names stay, because both are already in the record.

3. Coordinated institutional mobbing
What it names: the question whether the separate closures were only separate, or whether they functioned as one exclusion.
The record: the phrase is in the extracted tab names. The opened letters are separate instruments from separate offices. They do not contain one written order joining them. The sequence of those separate instruments is what makes the question real. The term stays because the sequence warrants the question.

4. Institutional betrayal
What it names: harm from an office that had the person, the complaint, or the duty, and did not examine the thing reported.
The record: non-allocation is not an investigation. A service restriction is not a finding that the complaint was false. The IR8415987 email of 1 July 2022 opens an item about a fatal-injury report. It is not the medical report. Closing or indexing the item is not the examination of the injury.

5. Administrative attrition
What it names: process repeated until the person is exhausted and the event is still unexamined.
The record: the demand for action states the exhaustion of explaining the same circumstances while the practical problems remain. The opened file is a chain of acknowledgements, non-allocations, extensions and restrictions, including the OAIC extension of 23 March 2022 and later access refusals.

6. Bureaucracy as attrition
What it names: the same exhaustion produced by ordinary procedure.
The record: each instrument has an ordinary administrative name. The harm identified is the accumulation.

7. Non-allocation
What it names: a decision not to allocate a disclosure or complaint for examination.
The record: Commonwealth Ombudsman notification of 22 March 2023, and the notification of 18 June 2023. These are the decisions. They are not findings that the allegations were false.

8. Service restriction
What it names: a limit placed on the person’s further use of the complaint pathway.
The record: the Commonwealth Ombudsman service-restriction document dated 20 January 2026, identified in the finding aid and in the demand for action.

9. Institutional silence
What it names: an office holding the complaint and not answering the event.
The record: non-allocation, restriction, and the statement on the targeting page that police, legal aid, the Ombudsman, the Prime Minister and OHCHR did not help.

10. Referral without resolution
What it names: a matter moved to another desk without the underlying event being decided.
The record: the institutional map in the final master report, and the opened letters that record receipt, referral or closure rather than examination of the reported conduct.

11. Institutional fragmentation
What it names: each office holding one piece, so that no opened file contains the whole life.
The record: DSS, the Federal Court correspondence, the Ombudsman, the NACC statement of 3 July 2023, the NDIA, the OAIC, ASIC, the AHRC, the NSW Trustee, police, and the NDIS Commission are all in the opened or indexed set. None of those single letters is the whole sequence.

12. Evidence fragmentation
What it names: the index, the email, or the filename standing in for the underlying document.
The record: the 1 July 2022 IR8415987 email is an automated opening notice. The finding aid warns that it is not the medical report. A transcript index entry for 15 April 2026 is not the original audio.

13. Administrative annihilation
What it names: the practical destruction of a person’s ability to be heard, housed, paid and believed, by accumulated administration rather than by one blow.
The record: the permanent term, the genocide-via-attrition page, and the opened cluster of money, housing, complaint closure and disability support.

14. Civil erasure
What it names: the person remaining a citizen while the practical means of acting as one are removed.
The record: the author’s description of internal displacement inside his own citizenship, alongside financial administration, complaint restriction and the absence of stable accommodation on the plan.

15. Civil death
What it names: the condition in which complaint, money, housing and belief have all been withdrawn, so that the person is socially and administratively treated as already gone.
The record: the targeting page puts the question in those words. The opened plan, the Trustee disposal notice, and the non-allocation notices are the documents against which the question is tested.

16. Social death
What it names: being made invisible, unheard and disbelieved while still alive.
The record: the testimony, the targeting page, and the consent declaration published with this reconstruction. The term is the name of that reported condition.

17. Social erasure
What it names: the removal of the person from professional, civic and relational life.
The record: the same pages, and the permanent term list.

18. Cumulative harm
What it names: separate decisions producing one injured life.
The record: 2021 medical event as reported, 2022 IR8415987 notice, 2023 disclosure handling, 2024 Trustee document, 2025 plan and vehicle disposal, 2026 accommodation, safety and complaint cluster. The dates are in the final master report and the forensic report.

19. Compounding harm
What it names: each later decision falling on a person already reduced by the earlier ones.
The record: the plan records no stable accommodation while approving a large sum. The vehicle notice removes transport. The complaint offices restrict or do not allocate. Each sits on top of the last.

20. Secondary victimisation
What it names: the complaint process itself becoming a further harm.
The record: the service restriction, and the demand that a person not have to prove a conspiracy before a service failure is examined.

21. Institutional retraumatisation
What it names: the person required to repeat the injury to offices that then do not examine it.
The record: the demand for action, in the author’s words, that he is exhausted by explaining the same circumstances while the problems remain.

22. Practical dependency
What it names: a formal right to leave that cannot be used, because money, transport, housing or support is controlled by someone else.
The record: the 1 June 2023 refusal of SDA and SIL with drop-in support approved instead; the 20 November 2025 plan recording no stable accommodation; plan management limited to WA South West Plan Managers; support coordination limited to Diversitas WA; the Trustee disposal of the vehicle.

23. Financial restriction
What it names: control of money and assets such that the person cannot use them for daily life.
The record: the section 122 document identified at 16 October 2024, and the Trustee letter that the Ford Territory, registration S797CDP, would be disposed of because requested evidence of how it was obtained had not been provided. The letter does not record a sale price.

24. Coercive financial control
What it names: financial administration used so that survival depends on the administrator’s discretion.
The record: the same Trustee cluster, and the published term page. The opened letter is the disposal notice. The full accounts were demanded and are not in the opened letter.

25. Poverty as disempowerment
What it names: the absence of food, money and medicine while a funded plan exists on paper.
The record: the plan total of $366,406.69, of which plan-managed Core is $274,255.84, and the humanitarian statement that food, medicine and a working phone were not in hand.

26. Impoverishment
What it names: the reduction to that condition.
The record: the same contrast between the approved figure and the reported lack of necessities.

27. Failure to protect
What it names: a safeguarding duty identified, and the protection not shown in the opened file.
The record: the indexed AbleCare call of 15 April 2026 concerning a reported death threat, and the question under section 73Z whether a reportable incident was notified. Anglicare safety assessments are identified at 1 December 2025 and 20 January 2026. The original audio was not the file that was opened.

28. Reportable incident
What it names: an incident the NDIS Act requires a registered provider to notify, including alleged serious harm in connection with supports.
The record: the call index and sections 73Y and 73Z as set out in the demand for action. The transcript’s existence in the index does not by itself prove the words. It puts the provider’s knowledge in issue.

29. Weaponisation of psychiatric history
What it names: a diagnosis used to avoid examining an external event.
The record: the author’s testimony that psychiatric history was used this way, and the administrative sequence in which external complaints were not allocated. The diagnosis does not answer the letter. The letter does not answer the diagnosis. Both remain.

30. Pathologisation
What it names: the conversion of a reported external event into a symptom.
The record: the phrase “ingrained delusions of persecution” is in the extracted phrases. The targeting page and the demand for action refuse that conversion.

31. Credibility substitution
What it names: the person’s psychiatric file put in place of the documentary file.
The record: the same refusal, and the forensic report’s separation of what an agency letter actually says from what a later essay claimed it said.

32. Reputational pre-emption
What it names: the person disbelieved before the record is read.
The record: the pattern of non-allocation and the testimony of collective discredit. The term names that order of events.

33. Scapegoating
What it names: one person carrying the blame, the risk, or the exclusion for a group.
The record: the targeting page puts scapegoating in issue beside social mobbing. The opened sequence shows the consequences landing on one life.

34. Whistleblower detriment
What it names: harm after a disclosure, where the disclosure is a reason for the harm.
The record: PID 2023/Krypton, the 27 March 2023 Federal Court correspondence, the March and June 2023 non-allocations, and the 3 July 2023 statement to the NACC. The term names the question the dates require. The opened Court letter is the Court’s letter. It is not a judgment on the whole disclosure.

35. Gaslighting
What it names: the person told that what he reported did not happen, was not received, or is his misperception.
The record: the extracted phrase “Your email has not been forwarded”, section 24A as an access refusal that documents do not exist, and the difference between what the author was told a letter said and what the opened letter says.

36. Institutional gaslighting
What it names: that denial when it comes from the office that holds the file.
The record: the same access and non-allocation instruments.

37. Moral injury
What it names: the wound of being betrayed by a body that was supposed to protect.
The record: the consent declaration and the testimony. The term names the injury described there, not a clinical label invented for this page.

38. Political exile
What it names: displacement inside the country of one’s own citizenship.
The record: the author’s published phrase, “internally displaced refugee within the democracy of my own citizenship”, and the targeting page.

39. Humanitarian emergency
What it names: the present need for food, medicine, housing, money, transport and Crystal’s care.
The record: the SOS pages and the demand for action. The plan figure does not cancel the reported absence of necessities.

40. Withholding of consent
What it names: the express refusal, published at the top of the demand for action, of permission to harm, erase, impoverish, silence or control him.
The record: that declaration, 13 November 2026.

41. Duty of care
What it names: the responsibility that follows from power over a vulnerable person.
The record: the declaration. Power over disability support, money, housing or complaint handling is not permission to inflict the harm.

42. No divine permission
What it names: the faith statement that no office is God and no office may destroy a life God has kept alive.
The record: the same declaration. It is testimony of faith. It is also the moral premise of the demands.

43. Conspiracy
What it names: a common purpose among the actors.
The record: the permanent term. The opened letters are separate. They do not contain one written common order. The allegation stands as the question the sequence raises. It is not withdrawn because a single joining letter was not in the opened set.

44. Corruption
What it names: the use of public or entrusted power for a purpose other than the duty.
The record: the permanent term, the PID, and the NACC statement. The term is in the list because those records put official conduct in issue.

45. Police corruption
What it names: that question directed at police conduct.
The record: police-matter documents dated 20 January 2026 are identified. The term is the author’s permanent term for what those documents and the testimony require to be answered.

46. Institutional complicity
What it names: separate offices producing one exclusion by what they did and what they refused to do.
The record: the opened sequence. Complicity here is the name of that joint result. It is not a sentence copied from a verdict.

47. Learned helplessness
What it names: the predicted collapse of a person’s effort after repeated action produces no change.
The record: the author’s statement that he is exhausted by repetition without action. The term names that described effect. It is not a diagnosis written on this page.

48. Total human betrayal
What it names: the author’s name for the whole, not for one office.
The record: the published term page and the testimony. The itemised entries above are the parts. This is the name he gives the sum.

The witness remains. The record remains. The terms above are the ones the opened records warrant.
