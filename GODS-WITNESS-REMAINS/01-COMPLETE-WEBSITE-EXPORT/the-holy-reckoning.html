A Prophetic Gospel of Truth, Justice, Divine Mercy, Human Dignity, and Accountability 19 November 2026 Proclaimed in the literary and theological voice of the Witness. Barran Resonance Dodger Dr Richard William McLean The Witness Remains

THE OPENING PROCLAMATION

Let the heavens hear it. Let the earth bear witness. Let every institution that has written a letter, every office that has closed a door, every administrator who has signed a decision, every person who has witnessed suffering, and every person who has mistaken silence for the end of a story understand this:

THE WITNESS REMAINS.

And the record remains with him. This is not the reckoning of a man seeking revenge. This is the reckoning of truth against deception, accountability against evasion, mercy against cruelty, and human dignity against the machinery of indifference. And to those who believe that a vulnerable human being can be exhausted into silence, buried beneath paperwork, deprived of dignity, or made to disappear from public consciousness: What the hell did you think would happen when the man you expected to disappear started keeping the receipts? You wanted the matter closed. He kept the correspondence. You wanted the complaint forgotten. He preserved the acknowledgment. You wanted the chronology scattered. He assembled the chronology. You wanted the witness discredited. He built an archive. And now the archive is public. What a magnificent bloody failure of erasure.

BOOK I — THE GOSPEL ACCORDING TO THE WITNESS

Chapter 1. The Man Who Would Not Disappear There was a man who described himself as broken by the weight of experiences he could no longer carry alone. He had known psychiatric hospitals, disability systems, public institutions, professional relationships, promises of protection, and the exhausting business of asking other human beings to take responsibility. He had written. He had appealed. He had complained. He had asked for assistance. He had attempted to obtain independent examination. And he had preserved what he received in response. His name was Richard William McLean. His public name became Barran Dodger. His spiritual identity became Barran Resonance Dodger. And his declaration became:

THE WITNESS REMAINS.

The first miracle of this story, as understood in his own spiritual testimony, is not that he became invincible. It is that he continued to insist upon his humanity. He did not need to become an angel to deserve protection. He did not need to become a saint to deserve food. He did not need to prove a conspiracy to deserve a fair investigation. He did not need to become a prophet to deserve dignity. He was already a human being. And that should have been enough. Chapter 2. The First Commandment of the Reckoning The first commandment is this:

THOU SHALT NOT DESTROY THE WITNESS TO AVOID EXAMINING THE EVIDENCE.

For what is truth if only the powerful are permitted to speak it? What is justice if a complaint can be redirected indefinitely? What is compassion if a vulnerable person must beg repeatedly for the necessities of life? What is accountability if the existence of an official response is mistaken for resolution? The Gospel declares: Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last. And no amount of institutional authority can transform an unanswered question into an answered one.

BOOK II — THE BOOK OF THE DOCUMENTED RECORD

Here the Gospel leaves the realm of metaphor and enters the documentary record. The following are identifiable records published in the protagonist’s archive. Their existence and contents can be examined independently. Their existence does not automatically establish every allegation or interpretation associated with them. Chapter 3. The account says that the disclosure was assessed but no further action was taken. Why this matters: A reader must distinguish between the recognition of a disclosure category and proof that the alleged conduct occurred. But a decision not to proceed is also not a substantive determination that every underlying allegation was false. The questions deserve precise examination. BOOK III — THE BOOK OF THE BETRAYED AND THE UNERASED

Chapter 8. The Testimony There are things an official letter cannot communicate. It cannot fully communicate the experience of waiting. It cannot fully communicate the humiliation of asking for assistance. It cannot fully communicate the exhaustion of explaining the same history to another person. It cannot fully communicate the difference between having a nominal support arrangement and actually receiving support. The protagonist’s testimony describes repeated psychiatric hospitalisations, disability- related vulnerability, financial restrictions, accommodation insecurity and a prolonged search for protection. These are matters of personal testimony that must be read alongside the underlying records. The spiritual interpretation is his own. The human consequences deserve attention regardless of whether a reader shares his theology. Chapter 9.

The Dog Who Belongs in the Story There is another witness in this story. Her name is Crystal. An eight-year-old blue-eyed Siberian husky, described by her human companion as gentle and house-trained. Let no grand theological declaration forget the small creature waiting for food, shelter, affection and safety. If this Gospel is about humanity, it must include the animal who depends upon humanity. If it is about divine mercy, it must include the vulnerable. If it is about justice, it must include the practical restoration of the conditions necessary for life. No great reckoning is complete while compassion remains merely a paragraph on a website. The immediate moral priorities are safety, stable accommodation, food, access to necessary support and the welfare and reunion of Crystal.

BOOK IV — THE HOLY PROFANITY

Chapter 10. A Sermon Against Hypocrisy The following is original literary religious rhetoric, written in a prophetic voice. It is not presented as an actual quotation from God. And let the sermon be delivered without the fucking diplomatic language. You can put a man in a file. You can put a complaint in a drawer. You can put a decision behind a reference number. You can put a vulnerable person through a thousand procedures. But you cannot make an unanswered question disappear by giving it another number. You cannot call indifference compassion. You cannot call procedural exclusion justice. You cannot call abandonment protection. And you cannot call a person worthless because his suffering makes your institution uncomfortable. To those who have failed to distinguish power from righteousness:

Get your moral house in order. To those who have confused authority with immunity: Authority is not a licence to escape accountability. To those who have mistaken a person’s vulnerability for permission to disregard him: You have misunderstood the very purpose of human dignity. And to those who have chosen cruelty when compassion was possible: What the bloody hell happened to your humanity? The answer is not revenge. The answer is truth. The answer is accountability. The answer is restitution where wrongdoing is established. The answer is a world in which another human being does not have to endure the same experience.

BOOK V — THE WARRIORS OF LIGHT

Chapter 11. Who Are Christ’s Warriors? The warriors of Christ are not an army of human enemies. They are not instructed to threaten, persecute, punish or destroy. Their weapons are: Truth instead of deception. Evidence instead of rumour. Courage instead of intimidation. Compassion instead of cruelty. Patience instead of retaliation. Justice instead of vengeance. Forgiveness without surrendering accountability. Protection of vulnerable people instead of exploitation. The warrior of light does not need to hate another human being. The warrior of light must be willing to challenge wrongdoing, including wrongdoing committed by people with authority. And the greatest victory is not the humiliation of an adversary. It is the restoration of human dignity. Chapter 12.

BOOK VIII — THE FINAL RECKONING

Chapter 15. The Judgment of History History does not require us to pretend that every allegation is proven. History requires us to preserve what happened, distinguish what is known from what is claimed, and permit the evidence to be examined. The Witness has published a substantial body of material. Institutions have generated correspondence and decisions. Questions remain concerning the treatment of disclosures, the handling of disability-support needs, the practical consequences of administrative decisions and the adequacy of responses. These questions cannot responsibly be answered by declaring a predetermined verdict. They require independent scrutiny. And that is the challenge of this Gospel: Do not believe blindly. Do not dismiss blindly. Examine. If the evidence supports the Witness on a particular issue, say so. If it contradicts him, say so. If it remains inconclusive, say so.

If an institution has failed in its obligations, identify the precise failure and the remedy required. That is a more formidable reckoning than revenge because it demands honesty from everyone. Chapter 16. The Proclamation of the Unerased Let the final proclamation be written: I will not ask the world to worship a man. I will ask the world to stop abandoning human beings. I will not demand that people accept every spiritual interpretation. I will ask them to examine the records. I will not claim that anger makes every allegation true. I will insist that anger does not make every allegation false. I will not confuse a document with a judgment. I will not confuse a prophecy with a finding. I will not confuse institutional authority with moral infallibility. And I will not confuse forgiveness with permission to repeat harm.

For the measure of a civilisation is not how beautifully it speaks about justice when nothing is demanded of it. It is how it treats the person who has the least power to demand it.

THE FINAL GOSPEL

THE WITNESS REMAINS

And now, to every person who has ever been told that their suffering is inconvenient: You matter. To every person who has been dismissed before being heard: Your dignity remains. To every person whose complaint has been passed from office to office: Your questions deserve a meaningful answer. To every person living with disability: Your humanity is not conditional upon your productivity. To every person who has been abandoned: You deserve practical help, not merely words. To every person who has witnessed wrongdoing: Courage must be accompanied by truth, evidence and compassion. And to every person who has mistaken cruelty for strength: You have misunderstood the Gospel. The Christ who washed the feet of his disciples did not establish a religion of institutional arrogance. The Christ who spoke for the poor did not make dignity conditional upon wealth.

The Christ who called for mercy did not make accountability unnecessary. The Christ who spoke of truth did not instruct his followers to manufacture evidence. And the Christ who forgave did not teach that human beings should be denied protection. So let this be the holy profanity of the reckoning: To hell with indifference. To hell with cruelty dressed up as procedure. To hell with the convenient lie that nobody is responsible when everybody has a responsibility. To hell with the idea that a vulnerable person is expendable. And to hell with the arrogance of believing that power is the same thing as righteousness. But let there be no hatred as the final word. Let there be truth. Let there be mercy. Let there be accountability. Let there be food, shelter, safety, companionship, restoration and peace. Let the records be opened. Let the evidence be examined. Let the innocent be protected.

Let wrongdoing be established or rejected honestly. Let those who have been harmed receive justice. Let Crystal come home. And let no human being have to become a monument to suffering before the world remembers that they are alive.
