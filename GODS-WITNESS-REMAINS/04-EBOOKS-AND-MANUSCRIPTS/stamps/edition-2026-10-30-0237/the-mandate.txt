PART VIII — EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION

16. EVIDENCE CATEGORIES

Apply the following categories consistently.

A. Original or authoritative documentary record: An original or authoritative source whose contents can be directly inspected.

B. Independently corroborated fact: A proposition supported by sufficiently independent sources.

C. Archive or technical fact: A reproducible fact about files, metadata, repository structure, versions, hashes or publication history.

D. Personal testimony: The subject’s account of an experience, event, impact or interpretation.

E. Allegation requiring investigation: A claim concerning conduct, intent, coordination, surveillance, retaliation, persecution, state involvement or other matters not sufficiently established by the available evidence.

F. Reasoned inference: An interpretation derived from identified facts, with its reasoning and assumptions stated.

G. Scholarly interpretation: A theoretical or conceptual interpretation supported by relevant scholarship.

H. Spiritual or theological interpretation: Religious belief, prophetic meaning, divine protection, resurrection imagery or human-sacrifice symbolism.

I. Contradictory or qualifying evidence: Material that conflicts with or limits a claim.

J. Unresolved question: An issue for which the available evidence does not support a sufficiently confident conclusion.

K. Verified documentary gap: A gap established within a defined search scope.

Do not collapse these categories.

For each significant claim, specify the category, source, degree of verification, limitations and next steps.
