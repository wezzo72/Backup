Legal Aid and the practical availability of justice 4.1 The access-to-justice paradox The central proposition is that a legal assistance system may exist to improve access to justice while its eligibility rules, funding limits, jurisdictional boundaries or individual decisions leave a particular person without practical assistance to pursue complex and consequential matters. That proposition is a legitimate subject for investigation. It is not automatically proof of an unlawful refusal or a systemic failure. The relevant questions are: What assistance was sought? Which service received the request? What legal issue was the assistance intended to address? What written decision was made? What statutory provisions, eligibility criteria or policies were applied? Was the decision reviewed, reconsidered or appealed? What alternative assistance was offered? Could the alternative realistically address the problem? Did the absence of assistance create a risk to procedural rights or limitation periods? 4.2 Governing legislation The principal statute is the Legal Aid Commission Act 1979 (NSW).
