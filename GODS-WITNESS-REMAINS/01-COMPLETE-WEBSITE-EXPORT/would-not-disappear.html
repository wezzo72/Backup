PART C — LEGAL AND HUMAN-RIGHTS FRAMEWORK

The following principles are stated at the level of general law and are included to frame the questions raised by the record.

They do not constitute legal advice, nor do they assert that any particular decision in this matter was unlawful.

Procedural fairness (natural justice)

Procedural fairness is a fundamental requirement of most administrative decision-making that affects a person's rights, interests or legitimate expectations. It ordinarily comprises the hearing rule (an opportunity to be heard) and the bias rule (decision by an impartial decision-maker). The content of the duty depends on the statutory context and the nature of the decision: a fair hearing will generally require notice of the issues, disclosure of credible and significant information, and a reasonable opportunity to respond. source

Judicial review and procedural fairness

The Australian Law Reform Commission notes that a person may seek judicial review of an administrative decision on the basis that procedural fairness was not observed. Courts examine whether a duty to afford procedural fairness arose and, if so, what its content required in the circumstances. In the absence of clear contrary legislative intent, administrative decision-makers must accord procedural fairness to those affected by their decisions. source

Disability Discrimination Act 1992

The Disability Discrimination Act 1992 makes discrimination on the ground of disability unlawful in defined areas of public life, including the administration of Commonwealth laws and programs. Its objects include eliminating disability discrimination and ensuring, as far as practicable, that people with disability have the same rights to equality before the law as the rest of the community. source

Reasonable adjustments

Under the DDA, reasonable adjustments are changes to a process, product or environment that remove barriers for people with disability. A failure to provide reasonable adjustments may amount to direct or indirect unlawful discrimination unless unjustifiable hardship is established. source

CRPD Article 13 — access to justice

Article 13 of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities requires States Parties to ensure effective access to justice for persons with disabilities on an equal basis with others, including through procedural accommodations, to facilitate effective participation in all legal proceedings, including investigative and other preliminary stages. source
