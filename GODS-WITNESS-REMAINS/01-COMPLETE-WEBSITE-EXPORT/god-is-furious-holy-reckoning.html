Instalment 1 of the publication. 17 November 2026. An examination, not a verdict. The command and the transcript are saved beside it.

God Is Furious — the Holy Reckoning

The Witness Remains. The Great Reckoning.

Barran Dodger wrote the commission. This examination does not belong to him, and it does not belong to any office named in the letters. The video supplies the order of the themes. The letters that were opened supply what can be said. What was not opened is named as not opened.

The video that was actually used

The foundational text is the video the author supplied, not a similar title from another day.

Title: Chosen One: GOD Is FURIOUS — They TORTURED The INNOCENT And He’s Demanding HONOR In Your Name.

Address: youtube.com/watch?v=Pe2o2KxW2ik.

Channel: The Attraction Code.

Published: 16 November 2026 at 19:07 GMT. That is the morning of 17 November 2026 in Sydney.

The timestamped transcript used here is the one pasted with the command and marked as a tactiq.io transcript. It runs from the opening line to the close, “Your honor is unstoppable,” and it contains fourteen numbered declarations. It was read for this instalment. It was not re-captured from the player line by line. The saved copy is the transcript. The command that governs the work is the integrated command.

The video is a general address to a listener it calls “chosen one.” It does not name Richard McLean, Barran Dodger, any Australian minister, any hospital, or any agency. It is a template of themes. It is not evidence of what happened in any particular life. Short lines are quoted so the theme can be identified. The rest is not copied.

What this voice will not do

This examination does not claim that God is angry, that the universe has issued a verdict, or that a delay in human affairs is a divine warrant. Those are the video’s theological and metaphorical claims. They are literary architecture. They are not findings.

The Holy Reckoning, as this work uses the words, is accountability without revenge, truth without fabrication, justice without prejudice, and humanity without exception. It is not a licence to condemn a person because a video says the scales have already crashed.

  2. Number one, from about nine minutes. Lies about a person are called an attempt to kill a name, a reputation, and an identity. Words are said to program perception. The speaker says the accused were writing their own record while they thought silence meant agreement.
  3. Number two, from about seventeen minutes. Mistreatment is described as mockery of a decision already made, envy toward a person who was chosen, and a tantrum that cannot change a scoreboard.
  4. Number three, from about twenty-six minutes. The desire is said to be ruin rather than reality, because innocence exposes guilt. A clean person in a room of thieves.
  5. Number four, from about twenty-nine minutes. Quiet is called surveillance and the stacking of evidence, not an excuse. A camera with the light on.
  6. Number five, from about thirty-three minutes. The people who thought they were testing the quiet person were exposing themselves.
  7. Number six, not yet answered here. Sacrifice of one person for the comfort of others. A scapegoat.
  8. Number seven, not yet answered here. A reputation built on someone else’s ruin, and a demand that what was taken be given back.
  9. Number eight, not yet answered here. Patience as letting a speaker contradict himself.

  11. Number ten, not yet answered here. Restraint mistaken for weakness.

  13. Number twelve, not yet answered here. Silence as the evidence, not as surrender.

The opening theme, set against the letters that were opened

The video’s first claim, in its own image, is that silence is not safety for the people who did the harm. “That silence right now is not protecting them.” It pictures a court in which the accused think the judge has stepped out, while a record is being kept.

What the opened letters show is narrower, and it is enough to answer the theme without borrowing the video’s certainty.

On 5 July 2023 a letter went to the Prime Minister. On 19 September 2023 A Riley, Security Law Section, Attorney-General’s Department, answered it as MC23-028244. The letter is printed on the plan page. It says the correspondence was referred to the Attorney-General, the Hon Mark Dreyfus KC MP, because the matters fell within his portfolio. It apologises for the delay. It says concerns about ASIO may be taken to the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security, and concerns about other Commonwealth agencies to the Commonwealth Ombudsman. It gives the websites and the Ombudsman’s number. It does not investigate either concern.

That is an official record of a referral. It is not an official record of a finding. The silence after a complaint, in this letter, is not an empty silence. It is a letter that sends the writer on. A referral is not the investigation the complaint asked for. The video’s courtroom image is a metaphor. The letter is the document. They are not the same thing. The letter does establish that the Prime Minister’s correspondence was received, numbered, delayed, and redirected.

The same pattern appears in a different office, in a different year, over a different subject. On 7 February 2022 Dr Richard McLean asked the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, in the words printed on the FOI page, “I request all information government agencies hold on me please.” On 14 April 2022 Petra Gartmann refused the request under section 24A(1)(b) of the Freedom of Information Act 1982, on the basis that reasonable steps had been taken and the documents could not be found or did not exist. The department also said it does not hold the records of other agencies, and it read the request down to its own documents.

A refusal because documents cannot be found is a search outcome. It is not a finding that the person who asked was wrong, and it is not a finding that no agency anywhere holds a record. On 8 October 2024 the same request was varied. The revised decision, FOI/2022/045IC, as printed on that page, says the department had told the Information Commissioner that some material might relate to the applicant and that there was not enough information for a positive match. The page says five documents were then released. A review, MR22/00677, is described as closed under section 54W(c). The reasons attachment is named as not being in the reading copy.

Set beside the video’s claim that “silence is surveillance,” the accurate documentary sentence is different. The department first said the documents were not there. It later said a positive match could not be made, and then it released five documents. The record of the asking, the refusal, the match problem, and the later release is public on this site. That is a file. It is not a hidden camera, and it is not a verdict.

Number one. A name, and what the bank’s letter does and does not say

The video’s first numbered declaration is that lies about a person can be an attempt to kill a reputation. It calls that “attempted murder of the soul.” That phrase is the video’s. It is not a legal charge, and it is not used here as one.

What can be examined is a letter about a name, a lock on an account, and a payment offered in exchange for an ending.

Reece, Manager, Critical and Complex Complaints, Group Customer Relations, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, wrote the outcome CF-15811059C on 2 November 2026. The email sending it is dated 3 November 2026. Both are printed on the bank outcome. The letter says NetBank access was incorrectly removed on 15 October 2026 as a once-off staff error, outside the bank’s Financial Management Order process, that the FMO team was not contacted, and that the trustee had not instructed the removal. Access, the letter says, was restored on 28 October 2026. The page dates the order at 22 July 2025.

The same letter offers $500 for the inconvenience, to be accepted by 9 November 2026, paid to the account ending 9778. The page says acceptance would treat the complaint as resolved and release the bank from further claims about the matter. The page also says the customer did not accept, and that the money was paid anyway. The offer and the condition are in the letter. The later payment, as a fact of the bank’s ledger, was not re-opened in this instalment. It remains the page’s account.

The letter closes two earlier complaints, CF-14935306C of 14 October 2025 and CF-14993212C of 13 November 2025, which concerned Australian Business Numbers said to be registered in the customer’s name. It says they will not be investigated again unless new information is provided. In the passages opened, it does not discuss the transfers the customer asked the bank to examine. A refusal to reopen is not a finding that the earlier story was false. It is a decision not to look again.

The video says a distorted picture was held up as the person. The bank letter does not say that. It says a staff error, a restoration, a payment, and a refusal to reopen. Those are the words that can be used. The customer’s further claims, including that the lockout and the ABN records are tied to an attempt on his life, are his account. They are not sentences of the letter.

The letter does tell him he may go to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority. That is an external door after an internal close. It is not, by itself, evidence that he walked through it.

Number two. A decision, and the difference between a referral and a scoreboard

The video’s second number says the harm was mockery of a decision the universe had already made. The image is a referee whose call the losing team cannot change.

No opened letter is a decision that a person was chosen by God, or that another person was rejected by God. This examination will not write that finding. The author’s theological claim that he is a witness remains his testimony. The archive does not independently prove divine selection.

What the letters do show is a different kind of decision, and the gap beside it.

The plan page sets three disability records side by side. An NDIS plan approval of 20 November 2025 is linked and said to record $366,406.69. An occupational therapy report by Georgia Gray of Allied Health 2U, 29 February 2024, is linked and said to recommend full-time formal support and Specialist Disability Accommodation with no other participants. An NDIA letter of 1 June 2023 is linked and said to state that he was not suitable for Supported Independent Living and not eligible for Specialist Disability Accommodation at that time. The PDFs were not re-opened for this instalment. The figures and the refusal are as that page states them. The author’s position, that the later funding should have followed the recommendation, is his position. It is not a sentence of the plan.

A plan that records a large sum, a clinical report that recommends one form of support, and an earlier letter that refuses that form, are three decisions. They do not agree. The disagreement is the document. It is not proof that anyone mocked a divine choice. It is proof, if the files say what the page says, that the administrative record contains both a recommendation and a refusal, and that they have not been reconciled in any letter this instalment opened.

Number three. Innocence as an allegation, not as a finding

The video’s third number says the guilty hate the innocent because the innocent is a mirror. “They wanted your ruin, not reality.”

Innocence, in a court, is not a mood. It is the absence of a proved offence, or a finding that an offence was not committed. No such finding was opened. No finding the other way was opened either.

The material that bears on a claimed attempt to destroy a reputation is the set of sentences printed in the letter to police of 3 November 2026. That letter says it has not been lodged by the page. It asks for records. It says it is not asking the police, in that letter, to find that an assassination was attempted. It prints sentences it attributes to messages from a person identified as Ben, including “Yes even the police said it was a close call,” “The agencies went back through phone records and believed she was paid to discredit you,” and “They made me sign a non disclosure agreement.”

Those sentences are a reported third-party statement, published as a request that police say whether they hold a matching record. They are not a police occurrence. The image files of the messages were not re-opened. No professional examination that authenticates them, and no professional examination that shows them to be fabricated, was opened. An absence of a rebuttal in the opened set is not proof that the event happened. An absence of an investigation is not a finding that it did not.

The video would call this the universe already holding the receipts. The accurate sentence is that the receipts, if they are the messages, have been published as a question to police, and the question has not been answered in any document this instalment read.

Number four. The quiet, and what was actually stacked

The video’s fourth number says the universe stayed quiet in order to stack evidence. “Silence isn’t absence. Silence is surveillance.”

Surveillance, as a technical claim about cameras, intercepts, or devices, is not established by a metaphor. Nothing opened in this instalment is a technical test of a device.

What was stacked, in the ordinary sense of a file, is a sequence of paper. A request for “all information” in February 2022, refused in April 2022, varied in October 2024. A letter to a Prime Minister in July 2023, answered by a referral in September 2023. A bank access removal on 15 October 2026, called a staff error, restored on 28 October, closed with an offer on 2 November. A police letter, written and not shown to have been lodged, asking whether a “close call” exists as a record.

The obligations page of 12 November 2026, which was read, adds names of further letters it did not reopen: a disclosure not allocated on 18 June 2023, an Ombudsman complaint 2021103546 and a later service restriction, a Law Enforcement Conduct Commission letter of 19 November 2025 described as out of jurisdiction, a National Anti-Corruption Commission statement of 3 July 2023, and an Australian Human Rights Commission contact of 4 July 2023. Those PDFs were not opened for this instalment. They are named. They are not read. A named letter can be part of a list. It cannot yet be part of a proved pattern beyond the fact that the site lists it.

The video says delayed judgment hits harder because denial has had time to settle. That is a psychological claim in the transcript. It is not a finding about any named official. What the papers support is that time passed between a complaint and a letter that did not decide the complaint. Delay is documented where a letter apologises for it, as MC23-028244 does. A purpose for the delay is not stated in that letter. A purpose must not be added.

Number five. Who was being examined

The video’s fifth number says the people who thought they were testing the quiet person were the ones under examination. “You were actually the mirror.”

A mirror, here, can be given a documentary meaning that does not require the video’s cosmology. The offices wrote on their own letterhead. The department wrote the refusal and later the release. The Attorney-General’s Department wrote the referral. The bank wrote the staff-error finding and the offer. Those sentences do not need the author’s interpretation in order to exist. That is the inversion the commission asked to be tested, and on these letters it holds in a limited way: the record these offices must account for is the record they wrote. It does not hold as a proof of guilt. A letter that refers is accounted for by being read as a referral. A letter that refuses access is accounted for by being read as a refusal. Neither letter, by being published, becomes a conviction of its author.

The author does not require an institution to validate his existence before these letters can be read. He also does not, by publishing them, validate every conclusion he draws from them. Both of those sentences are required by the same standard.

I left because I wanted to live, and my absence is not consent, are the author’s lines. They are testimony. They are printed because the commission requires them in the work, not because a letter has adopted them.
