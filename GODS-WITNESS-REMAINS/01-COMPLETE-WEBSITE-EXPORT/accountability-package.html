A. Legal and human-rights map

These are the frameworks a reader can test the file against. Naming a statute is not a finding that it was breached.

  • Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013 (Cth). The hosted DSS letter says an authorised officer reviewed the 6 and 10 July 2023 material under section 26 and did not allocate it, because a disclosure under that Act is made by a public official and the department said he was not one. That letter is a decision. It does not decide whether the underlying account is true. The Federal Court assessment of 27 March 2023 and the 18 June 2023 non-allocation notice are the same class of record: process, not a verdict on the facts he reported.
  • NDIS Act 2013, sections 73ZA to 73ZD. A person receiving NDIS supports has a different disclosure path from the Public Interest Disclosure Act. The question for the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission is whether a complaint about a provider, attendance, identification, and the use of funds was handled. His account of Able Point and Rachel is his account until the Commission’s file is read beside it.
  • Disability Discrimination Act 1992 and the Australian Human Rights Commission. The question is whether a person with a disability was treated less favourably, or whether a service failed to make adjustments. His disability and his history of advocacy are part of his testimony. A discrimination finding has not been issued in the records named on this page.
  • Commonwealth Ombudsman. The right question is maladministration: delay, referral without a decision, and whether a complaint was examined. The Attorney-General’s letter itself points him there, and to the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security for the ASIO concern. A pointer is not an investigation.
  • Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security. Jurisdiction is the legality and propriety of intelligence agencies, not the whole of government.
  • New South Wales. Health Care Complaints Commission for a hospital complaint. Law Enforcement Conduct Commission for a police-conduct complaint. NSW Ombudsman and the Independent Commission Against Corruption for the matters inside their statutes. None of those bodies is claimed here to have upheld the account.
  • International standards, as questions. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights. The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, in particular the rights to life, freedom from cruel treatment, liberty, a fair hearing, privacy, expression, and equality. The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. The Convention against Torture is engaged only if the legal definition of torture is met. His description of his conditions is testimony. It is not, by itself, a finding under that Convention. The OHCHR file is a receipt of a submission, number UR/UST/23/AUS/17.
  • Media standard. Accuracy, a right of reply, no gratuitous detail of a suicide method, and a correction when a quote is wrong.
