C. How to read the evidence

For the purposes of this testimony, the following distinctions are important:

  • Primary record: an original decision, letter, email, official document or other record that can be examined directly.

  • Personal testimony: my account of events, circumstances, conversations, consequences and experiences.

These categories do not diminish my testimony. They make it easier for readers to identify what each source can establish and where further documentary detail is needed.

D. Digital integrity and preservation

My archive uses preservation methods that may include file inventories, hashes, timestamp receipts and versioned repositories. Their evidentiary value depends on the specific record and on whether the result can be checked.

A SHA-256 hash is a digital fingerprint of a particular file. Matching hashes can support the conclusion that two files are identical at the byte level. A mismatch indicates that the files differ, though it does not by itself explain why.

An OpenTimestamps receipt may help establish that a submitted hash was committed to a timestamping system. A receipt’s status must be checked before making a claim about confirmation. A pending receipt should not be described as a confirmed Bitcoin timestamp.

Neither a hash nor a timestamp automatically proves the truth of a document’s contents. Their purpose is narrower: to assist with file integrity, identity and the preservation history of digital material.
