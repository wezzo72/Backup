IV. THE TEN IMMOVABLE STATEMENTS

A DEMAND FOR ACCOUNTABILITY, HISTORICAL TRUTH AND HUMAN ACTION

This is my testimony. This is my accusation. This is my documentary record. I am not asking the powerful to manufacture a history that is convenient to them. I am demanding that they confront the record that exists, the decisions documented within it, the consequences I have experienced, and the human being who remains behind the paperwork.

My archive is publicly accessible. The Barran Dodger documentary archive, its exhibit catalogue, its institutional and entity register, and its public-interest disclosure register provide starting points for examining the documents, decisions, correspondence and chronology underlying my testimony.

1. THE RECORD EXISTS. IGNORING IT DOES NOT MAKE IT DISAPPEAR.

I have assembled a documentary record of institutional correspondence, formal decisions, disclosure processes, complaints and refusals. The evidence is not made nonexistent because the person presenting it is distressed, angry, poor, disabled, isolated or unwilling to remain silent.

The Barran Dodger exhibit catalogue identifies documentary material; the institutional register provides a route through the entities appearing in that material; and The Documentary Trap sets out my account of the contradictions and consequences I see in the administrative record.

My position is unequivocal: the record must be examined on its contents, not dismissed because of the identity, circumstances or anger of the person who assembled it.

2. ADMINISTRATIVE AUTHORITY IS NOT IMMUNITY FROM ACCOUNTABILITY.

An official letter is a record of a decision. A refusal is a record of a refusal. A rejection is a record of a rejection. None becomes an unquestionable declaration of justice merely because it carries an institutional name.

The archive includes material concerning my Comcare determination of 26 May 2021, my Comcare claim dated 25 May 2021, and the wider exhibit catalogue.

The Comcare refusal is the evidence. E13, claim 1326583, 25 May 2021. E12, the determination that rejected me, 26 May 2021. They had the claim. They refused it. The Comcare path.

I demand accountability for the exercise of public power. Administrative procedure must not become a shield against scrutiny, and official authority must not be confused with moral infallibility.

3. DISTRESS DOES NOT CANCEL EVIDENCE, INTELLIGENCE OR HUMAN RIGHTS.

My anger is not a substitute for evidence. Neither is it a reason to ignore evidence. My emotional state does not erase correspondence, change the wording of a decision or make a dated document disappear.

My Personal Statement of Exile records my first-person account. My Statement on the Architecture of Erasure sets out my interpretation of the institutional and personal consequences I describe.

I am entitled to be heard as a human being and to have the documents I present examined on their merits. Distress is not a licence to disregard a person’s evidence, dignity or rights.

4. THE CUMULATIVE EFFECT MATTERS.

My case cannot be understood adequately by isolating every letter, rejection, referral or administrative decision from the history surrounding it. The sequence matters. The chronology matters. The relationship between events matters. So does the cumulative effect on the person required to endure them.

The institutional register, exhibit catalogue and public-interest disclosure register are entry points for examining that wider documentary sequence. The Documentary Trap sets out my interpretation of how administrative processes and unanswered issues fit together.

I call the cumulative experience administrative annihilation, civil erasure and social death.

A person’s life cannot be reduced to a collection of disconnected file numbers when the consequences unfold across years. The whole chronology deserves serious examination.

Start with the exhibit catalogue. Follow the entries to the available primary documents. Read the Comcare determination alongside the underlying claim document. Examine the public-interest disclosure register and the institutional register.
