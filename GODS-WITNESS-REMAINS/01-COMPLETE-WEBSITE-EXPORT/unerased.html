Bringing together decades of government correspondence, tribunal and administrative records, publicinterest disclosure material, disability and health documentation, employment history, published works, personal testimony, and spiritual writing, the archive transforms an individual’s contested life history into a publicly examinable evidentiary record.

The paper argues that forensic archives can redistribute evidentiary power between individuals and institutions by preserving records that might otherwise be deleted, overlooked, or reduced to an allegedly unreliable person’s account. EXPLAIN THE ROLE OF THE FORENSIC ARCHIVE AS A TOOL FOR ACCOUNTABILITY

The forensic archive(s) functions as a primary mechanism for institutional accountability by transforming a personal history into an independently verifiable, distributed, and preserved evidentiary record.

CORE FUNCTIONS OF THE FORENSIC ARCHIVE FOR ACCOUNTABILITY

Primary-Source Verification

Primary-Source Verification The archive compiles primary-source government correspondence, tribunal filings, Freedom of Information (FOI) releases, Public Interest Disclosure (PID) records, and medical or financial documentation. This allows claims to be evaluated against the State’s own documented record rather than relying solely on memory or assertion.

Separation of Evidence from Interpretation By structurally separating objective documentary evidence, such as agency decisions and correspondence, from personal testimony, subjective experiences, and spiritual writing, the archive provides a clear evidentiary matrix for objective review by third parties, such as lawyers or oversight bodies.

Mitigation of Resource Asymmetries Institutional systems frequently present a structural imbalance where government agencies possess superior legal and administrative resources compared to individual claimants. The archive serves as an evidentiary baseline—giving the individual a structured and preserved record with which to challenge decisions and narratives.

Prevention of Erasure and Suppression Utilising cryptographic tools such as SHA-256 hashes, Git blob hashes, and OpenTimestamps, the archive establishes byte-for-byte provenance and timestamped authenticity. This distributed, multi-location digital imprint reduces reliance on any single storage location and can help preserve the historical record against single-point deletion, suppression, or retroactive modification.

Illumination of Cumulative Patterns While single administrative decisions may appear minor or isolated when considered individually, assembling decades of files into a single chronological archive can highlight cumulative institutional patterns, jurisdictional gaps, and systematic administrative failures.

Public and Historical Scrutiny Serving as a public finding aid, the archive allows external researchers, journalists, advocates, and parliamentarians to scrutinise institutional practices, evaluate agency conduct, and investigate questions of systemic accountability.
