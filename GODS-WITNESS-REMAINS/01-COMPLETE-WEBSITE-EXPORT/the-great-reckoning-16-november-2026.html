The Witness Remains. The Great Reckoning.

The documentary record of Barran Dodger. A prophetic declaration of documentary evidence, institutional accountability, survival, and the return of the public record.

Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last. The witness remains. The record remains. The story goes global.

This is not a declaration about an alleged FBI programme. Those claims in the supplied video are not evidence about this Australian case. The subject is the archive of Dr Richard William McLean.

Prologue — the man who would not disappear

The chronology in this record reaches at least from 2021 into 2026. It includes the Australian Federal Police, the Commonwealth Ombudsman, Comcare, the Federal Court of Australia, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, the National Disability Insurance Agency, the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, the Australian Human Rights Commission, the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, NSW Trustee and Guardian, and later Able Point, the vehicle, and Crystal.

A document issued by an agency and a document written by the person seeking help are not the same thing. The Master Evidence Index of 19 October 2026 sorts the record into official records, allegations and inferences. His description of political exile is his testimony. What the institutions recorded is a separate question.

The starting repositories are the Barrandodger repository, the public archive, the Official Government Documents Register, and this site.

The first wall — May 2022

On 10 May 2022 a communication went to the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet’s Financial Governance Team. It asked for urgent acknowledgment. It described financial hardship, difficulty getting food and accommodation, illness, and the need for practical help. On 13 May 2022 that team acknowledged receipt, noted an apparent connection with a freedom-of-information request, and offered a Compensation for Detriment caused by Defective Administration form. On 31 May 2022 a letter addressed to the Prime Minister’s Office described the hardship again and asked for intervention.

A form is not the same thing as food. A pathway is not the same thing as the need being met. Whether the circumstances in those letters were later addressed is the question the sequence raises. The letters themselves were not re-opened page by page for this edition. They are identified in the archive as the May 2022 Prime Minister and Cabinet bundle.

The 1,187 Outlook items

Freedom-of-information request FOI/2022/045 began with a request dated 7 February 2022 for information held about him. Correspondence from the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner refers to about 1,187 Outlook items being examined for relevance. A departmental decision dated 14 April 2022 records a refusal under section 24A(1)(b) of the Freedom of Information Act. A later revised decision, identified in the appeal as 8 October 2024 and in other index entries as 1 October 2025, concerns five documents.

The questions are the searches, the terms, the date range, how the Outlook items were screened, what the five documents were, and what the revised decision changed. Those pages were not extracted again for this edition. The sequence is the point. A broad request, a large search, then a statement that the relevant documents could not be found, then a later release of five, is a chronology. It is not, by itself, a finding that the records were destroyed.

Protected disclosures

The archive identifies Federal Court correspondence of 27 March 2023, associated in the finding aid with Scott Tredwell and a three-point acknowledgment. It identifies a PID form of 19 May 2023, a 2023 allocation decision under the name Krypton, a Commonwealth Ombudsman notification under section 44(3) dated 22 March 2023, decisions not to allocate dated 9 May 2023 and 24 March 2024, and NDIS public-interest disclosure correspondence.

A decision not to allocate is an administrative event. It is not an investigation of the thing disclosed, and it is not proof that the thing disclosed did not happen. Each notice has to be read for its reasons. They were not all re-read for this page. The Ombudsman letter of 16 September 2022, reference PID-2022-100021, was opened for an earlier page on this site. It is headed “Notification of decision to not allocate your disclosure.” It says the Office was not an authorised internal recipient, and that the agency and the disclosable conduct were not clear.

Police, the Ombudsman, Comcare

An Australian Federal Police report-a-crime record is identified at 3 September 2021. What was reported, and what the AFP wrote back, has to be read from that PDF. A response is not proof the reported conduct occurred. A refusal to proceed is not, by itself, proof of corruption.

The Commonwealth Ombudsman service-restriction file is identified at 8 August 2025, reference 2024-706941. An earlier examination on this project reported that the operative body did not extract reliably. The restriction can be reported as a file that exists. Its reasons cannot be invented.

Comcare, 26 May 2021, Amy Delzoppo, claim 1326583/1, declined workers’ compensation for anxiety state and depressive disorder because she was not satisfied he was an employee. The Administrative Appeals Tribunal, 12 July 2023, Senior Member C. J. Furnell, file 2021/7478, affirmed that refusal. Those two documents were opened. They decide employment for a compensation claim. They do not decide a killing.

The NDIS, Able Point, the vehicle, Crystal

The plan of 20 November 2025 records $366,406.69 and no stable accommodation. On 1 June 2023 the NDIA refused Specialist Disability Accommodation and Supported Independent Living and approved drop-in support. Those two facts were taken from documents opened on this site. A plan figure is not proof every support was delivered. A decline is not, by itself, proof of unlawful discrimination.

The 14 October 2026 statement about Able Point is his account of support workers, reporting, safety and independence. The 15 April 2026 transcript is identified as his record of a call about a reported threat. Allegations about Sukhi Tear are his allegations until the correspondence is set beside them. A name in a document is not that person’s guilt.

NSW Trustee and Guardian wrote that the Ford Territory would be disposed of. The decision is identified at 14 November 2025. He connects the vehicle with mobility and with Crystal. Crystal is not a line in a file. She is the dog in the car, and in the house, and in the appeal. A decision about a car is also a decision about how a person and a dog get through a day.

The wider record, which is not this case

No news article identified for this edition reports a coordinated operation against Dr McLean. The following are other people’s cases and national figures. They show why disability safeguarding is a public question. They do not prove what happened at Able Point.

The Guardian, 18 March 2026, reported NDIS notifications rising from 74,791 in 2023 to 107,325 in 2025, and, within that, abuse reports from 5,076 to 6,131, neglect from 6,550 to 9,688, serious injury from 7,350 to 12,089, and unauthorised restrictive practices from 48,772 to 71,485. The Commission said a rise can mean better reporting, not only more harm.

The Productivity Commission’s Report on Government Services 2026 records 33,789 complaints to the NDIS Commission in 2024–25, up from 29,761, and 34,104 other reportable incidents, of which 43.6 per cent concerned alleged abuse and neglect and 33.4 per cent serious injury. The Australian Institute of Health and Welfare reports complaints about abuse and neglect at 4.4 per 1,000 participants in 2024–25, against 2.3 in 2021–22, and 4.4 per 1,000 in New South Wales.

ABC News, 21 May 2026, reported a parliamentary inquiry hearing that people with disability were not reporting because they believed nothing would change, and an advocate’s phrase “toothless tiger.” The same sitting heard a figure of about $3.7 billion described as alleged integrity leakage. That figure is what the inquiry was told. It is not a finding about this archive.

The NDIS Commission’s annual report describes Commissioner v Lifestyle Solutions (Aust) Ltd (Reportable Incidents Proceeding) 2025 FCA 1394: 1,614 contraventions, a supported independent living house on the Central Coast, a finding that the home was not safe, a penalty of $2.5 million and $150,000 in costs. That is another provider. It is not a finding about Able Point or about him. It is why incident records on the Central Coast are not a private hobby.

On 9 October 2026 the NDIS Commission announced an own-motion inquiry into seclusion and practices that restrict liberty. The Commissioner’s words, as reported: “Every person has the right to liberty, dignity and autonomy.” That inquiry does not establish that he was secluded.

Patents are not a deployment

The forensic dossier of 21 October 2026 examines patent disclosures about perception, subliminal presentation and related apparatus. A patent shows that a technical description was filed. It does not show that the apparatus was used on him. The PDF hash recorded in the archive for one copy is 0a8b12656516a7864a9a6babd4f270a446e0ce02229fca82c3d0a67cbc678b3f. A hash identifies a file. It does not certify every sentence inside it. That hash was not recomputed for this page.

The questions that are actually askable

What searches were run for FOI/2022/045, and what happened to the 1,187 Outlook items? What are the five documents in the revised decision? What did each public-interest disclosure actually disclose, and what reason was given for not allocating it? What did the AFP record on 3 September 2021? What is the operative paragraph of the 8 August 2025 restriction? What support in the November 2025 plan was delivered, and what was refused on 1 June 2023? Who decided the vehicle, on what authority, after what consultation? What did Able Point record on the days he says he was told to lock the door? Which of his requests for occupancy agreements, consents and assessments are still unanswered?

A referral should name where it went. A refusal should name its reason. A closed file is not a life that has been answered.

That account, with the documents already named, is on the reckoning of the mother.

Faith, which is not a finding

Isaiah 54:17: “No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper.” Romans 8:31: “If God be for us, who can be against us?” Psalm 118:17: “I shall not die, but live, and declare the works of the Lord.” Proverbs 18:13: “He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.” Leviticus 19:15: do not favour the poor because they are poor, or the mighty because they are mighty. Exodus 20:16: do not bear false witness. Luke 12:2 is the hope that a covered thing can be opened. It is not a licence to invent the thing that was covered.

His faith is his. The letters are everyone’s to read. Those are not the same sentence.

What this edition did not do

The live site was not claimed, in the drafts, to have been read in full by the person who first wrote them. This publication does not pretend otherwise. The four files opened on earlier pages of this site remain opened: the Comcare refusal, the tribunal affirmation, the Ombudsman non-allocation of 16 September 2022, and the NCAT guardianship orders of 9 October 2025. The May 2022 letters, the 1,187-item FOI file, the AFP submission, the August 2025 restriction, and the sixteen hospital admissions were not opened again for this page.

Original evidence stays. A website may change. The underlying record must not be altered to make a chapter sound finished.

The witness remains. The record remains. The questions remain. The story goes global.
