1. PURPOSE: THIS REPORT MUST CHANGE WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

I am publishing this report because I need the circumstances documented in my evidence archive to be examined and acted upon.

This is not simply a personal history. It is not merely a collection of complaints. It is not an academic exercise in describing institutional processes while the person affected continues to experience their consequences.

It is a humanitarian demand for intervention, an evidence-based request for independent investigation, and a formal call for accountability and remedy.

I allege that a prolonged combination of administrative decisions, disability-support failures, financial restrictions, inadequate protection, complaint-handling failures and institutional inaction has contributed to poverty, dependency, displacement, loss of independence and serious harm to my dignity and wellbeing.

I want these matters investigated. I want any wrongdoing established by the evidence exposed. I want the consequences addressed. I want my independence restored.

I do not ask anyone to accept an allegation without evidence. I demand that evidence be examined rather than dismissed, ignored, fragmented or indefinitely referred elsewhere.

The purpose of this report is not to obtain another acknowledgement. The purpose is to obtain action.

Its objectives are:

* Immediate humanitarian protection and practical assistance.
* Restoration of access to money and financial independence.
* Safe, appropriate and stable accommodation.
* Delivery of disability supports consistent with assessed needs and applicable obligations.
* Independent examination of provider conduct and safeguarding.
* Examination of financial administration and asset disposal.
* Investigation of public-interest disclosures and any alleged detriment.
* Independent assessment of institutional decisions and possible misconduct.
* Proper calculation and consideration of financial loss, restitution and compensation.
* Public accountability based on verifiable evidence.

A person should not have to complete years of complaint correspondence before obtaining food, safe accommodation or access to essential funds.

A person should not have to establish criminal wrongdoing before a provider’s safeguarding responsibilities are examined.

A person should not have to prove a coordinated institutional scheme before an identifiable service failure is addressed.

A person should not have to surrender independence in order to receive disability support.

These requests are not findings of legal liability. They are the practical interventions required to address the circumstances I have reported and to establish what assistance is available immediately.

4. NDIS: SUPPORTS, SAFEGUARDING AND INDEPENDENCE

The NDIS is central to this report because the author describes significant disability-support needs and a substantial gap between funded or assessed support and the practical assistance he says he received.

The author reports an NDIS plan dated 20 November 2025, with total funding of $366,406.69. The reported categories include Core Flexible supports, Behaviour Support, Improved Daily Living, Improved Living Arrangements and Support Coordination/Psychosocial Recovery Coaching. The original plan must control the exact amounts, conditions, stated goals and funding periods.

A plan’s approved funding is not proof that the services were delivered. Nor does the existence of a service agreement prove that the service was safe, sufficient or consistent with the participant’s needs.

The examination must compare four things:

* What the participant’s assessments said he needed.
* What the NDIA approved.
* What providers agreed to deliver.
* What was actually delivered.

4.1 Provider incident management

Section 73Y of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act requires registered providers to implement and maintain an appropriate incident-management system. Section 73Z establishes the framework for notification and management of reportable incidents. The current Act and rules must be applied to the date of each event. legislation.gov.au +1

The NDIS Commission describes reportable incidents as including events that have happened, or are alleged to have happened, in connection with the delivery of NDIS supports. It states that registered providers must notify the Commission of reportable incidents. NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission +1

This is directly relevant to the 15 April 2026 call transcript identified in the archive.

The investigation must establish:

* The exact words used in the report to the provider CEO.
* The date and time the concern was communicated.
* Whether the provider recorded the concern.
* Whether it was assessed under the provider’s incident-management system.
* Whether it met the applicable reportable-incident definition.
* Whether notification was made to the Commission.
* Whether appropriate safety measures were considered.
* Whether the participant was informed of the response.
* Whether any investigation occurred.
* Whether the provider preserved the original call recording and associated records.

The allegation that the provider failed to report a reportable incident must be tested against the incident’s actual nature, the provider’s knowledge, its registration status and the applicable rules. The existence of a transcript does not alone establish a breach.
