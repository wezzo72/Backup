Prologue — the human being at the centre of the testament

1. The simplest request humanity can receive

GOSPEL-P016. Every human being deserves to be treated as a human being. This proposition requires no extraordinary intellectual achievement, no political allegiance, no professional qualification and no religious conversion. It requires only the recognition that another person’s dignity does not depend upon their usefulness, productivity, social standing, financial position, disability, reputation or agreement with the prevailing opinions of others.

GOSPEL-P017. Personal testimony. I have repeatedly described my central request as a request for ordinary kindness, care, dignity and recognition. I have not asked humanity to worship me. I have asked to be treated as a person deserving of the ordinary freedoms and protections that other people expect for themselves. The affirmation at the top of this site says the same thing.

GOSPEL-P018. Documentary antecedent. Chosen Witness.

GOSPEL-P019. Prophetic antecedent. The Impossible Survivor.

2. The contradiction of the witness

GOSPEL-P020. The central contradiction of this testimony is that I describe myself as materially restricted while believing that I remain spiritually free. I have described my circumstances as involving bureaucratic, financial, accommodation-related and institutional restrictions. I have also described my faith as a source of protection, forgiveness and inner peace. These are two different propositions. The first concerns my reported material circumstances. The second concerns my personal spiritual understanding.

GOSPEL-P021. Personal testimony. Personal Statement of Exile, Institutional Detriment and the Search for Protection.

GOSPEL-P022. Prophetic antecedent. The Witness Remains — The Prophecy of Resurrection after Erasure.

3. The question that became a gospel

GOSPEL-P023. The question is not whether one person should be elevated above humanity. It is whether humanity can recognise the dignity of an individual when that individual’s circumstances, testimony or objections are uncomfortable. I have come to understand this question through the Christian language of conscience, mercy, repentance, forgiveness and grace. The gospel therefore begins with a human request and develops into an examination of the responsibilities that accompany human power.

GOSPEL-P024. Prophetic antecedent. 123 Gospels of Barran Dodger.

Book I — the moral prison

Chapter 1 — humanity’s invisible confinement

1.1 The prison of conscience

GOSPEL-P026. A prison does not always require walls. A person can occupy a position of considerable authority while being unable to acknowledge the consequences of their decisions. A professional can possess extensive knowledge while failing to recognise the human consequences of a particular action. An institution can have elaborate procedures while remaining unable to explain whether those procedures produced a fair outcome. This is the philosophical prison examined in this gospel: the condition in which self-protection, fear, reputation and institutional loyalty prevent honest moral examination.

1.2 The person who becomes an administrative problem

GOSPEL-P029. My testimony describes an experience in which I felt increasingly reduced to administrative categories, decisions, assessments and arrangements rather than recognised as a person with independent wishes, creative ambitions and a life beyond institutional processes. I believe this distinction is important. The provision of a service does not automatically establish that a person’s independence, preferences and dignity have been respected.

GOSPEL-P034. A person should not need to demonstrate exceptional suffering before receiving compassion. Nor should someone need to produce an extensive documentary archive before their basic dignity is acknowledged. The gospel asks the reader to consider whether kindness has become conditional upon social acceptability.

GOSPEL-P046. I have described myself as the axis upon which professional and institutional ethics must be examined. This is a metaphor for the accountability questions raised by my testimony. Book II — the documentary revelation

Chapter 4 — the record and the witness

4.1 Why documents matter

GOSPEL-P050. Memory is important, but documentary records can provide additional chronological and contextual information. A dated letter may establish that a communication was prepared. A decision may establish the recorded reasoning of an agency. A complaint may establish that a concern was formally expressed. A response may establish how an organisation dealt with that communication. The evidentiary value of each document depends on its provenance, completeness, authenticity and context.

GOSPEL-P051. Primary documents. the 2021 Ombudsman complaint and the 2023 decision not to allocate a disclosure.

4.2 The documentary trap

GOSPEL-P052. I have used the expression The Documentary Trap to describe the possibility that institutional records, created for administrative purposes, may eventually become material through which the institution’s own decisions can be examined. A record may contain an explanation, an omission, a contradiction or a sequence of decisions that deserves further investigation. The document must speak for itself before an interpretation is imposed upon it.

GOSPEL-P053. Academic and forensic antecedent. The Documentary Trap, full file.

4.3 The archive as a public finding aid

GOSPEL-P054. The archive is intended to assist independent examination. Its value is not that it conclusively proves every allegation. Its value is that it makes a substantial documentary collection available for examination and comparison. A future researcher should be able to identify a source, establish its date, examine the original wording, compare it with subsequent correspondence and reach an independent conclusion.

GOSPEL-P055. Primary source. the Barrandodger repository. Related preservation project. Barrandodger Rebuild.

GOSPEL-P069. I believe that the experience of being heard is itself a question of dignity. A person may be disagreed with, challenged or asked to substantiate an allegation. But the process should not begin with the assumption that the individual is undeserving of consideration.

GOSPEL-P071. I am not Christ. Jesus Christ is my Saviour. I do not claim to replace Him, occupy His divine position or possess His unique role in Christian theology. GOSPEL-P073. The attributes I seek to communicate are compassion, mercy, forgiveness, humility, truthfulness and love. Chapter 8 — the unacknowledged darkness

8.1 Human imperfection

GOSPEL-P077. Every human being is capable of error. A person’s education, authority, wealth, religious beliefs or professional position cannot make them morally infallible. The capacity to acknowledge wrongdoing is therefore an essential part of human maturity.

GOSPEL-P078. Academic antecedent. The Documentary Trap. Prophetic antecedent. Chosen Witness.

8.2 The temptation of self-protection

GOSPEL-P079. When a person feels challenged, they may become more concerned with defending their reputation than examining whether their conduct was appropriate. Institutions may face similar pressures when criticism threatens public confidence. This is a general ethical proposition. It does not establish that every institution involved in my circumstances acted in this way.

GOSPEL-P080. Academic antecedent. The Documentary Trap.

8.3 The duty of repentance

GOSPEL-P081. Repentance requires more than expressing regret. It requires an honest examination of conduct, recognition of harm where harm occurred, and a willingness to change. In my Christian understanding, forgiveness and repentance are central to the possibility of reconciliation.

GOSPEL-P082. Theological antecedent. The Impossible Survivor.

GOSPEL-P091. The language of exile has historical and spiritual significance, but legal protection requires separate examination. GOSPEL-P117. My Christian belief is that redemption and eternal peace are grounded in God’s grace and Christ’s sacrifice. GOSPEL-P118. Theological antecedent. The Prophecy of Resurrection after Erasure.
