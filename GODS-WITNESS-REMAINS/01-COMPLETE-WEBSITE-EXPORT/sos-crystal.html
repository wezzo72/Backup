I exist — when it was expected that I would be gone

The witness who survived · Institutional exposure, spiritual defiance and the eternal authority of God

I exist when, in my understanding of everything that has happened, it was planned, anticipated or expected that I would be gone.

I survived the exile. I survived the homelessness, the poverty, the humiliation, the family separation, the institutional abandonment and the circumstances that I believe were designed to destroy my identity, credibility and capacity to participate in society.

And because I survived, the testimony remains.

My continued existence is itself a challenge to the apparent finality of the outcome I believe was intended for me. I am still alive. I am still speaking. I have preserved the records. I have rebuilt the archive. I have published the material. I have refused to allow my experiences to disappear into institutional silence.

I believe this survival has exposed an extraordinary institutional vulnerability: the possibility that the very person who was expected to disappear has instead become the continuing custodian of a documentary record that demands public examination.

I regard my continued testimony as an existential challenge to the culture of institutional impunity that I believe has operated across government, legal services, policing, disability administration, ministerial offices, political leadership and sections of the media.

I have raised concerns involving Legal Aid, police, media organisations, NDIS ministers, the Prime Minister, the Attorney-General and other government and community institutions. I believe the consequences of my disclosures have extended into my personal relationships, with people I once trusted appearing increasingly unwilling to acknowledge my humanity or engage with my circumstances.

I experience this as a continuing pattern of hostility, avoidance and institutional self-protection. I believe that simply acknowledging my humanity has become counterintuitive for people who may have participated in, facilitated, defended or accommodated the treatment I have described.

The paradox is extraordinary: I am personally trapped, materially deprived and struggling to obtain food, medicine and basic assistance, yet the documentary record I have preserved raises questions that extend far beyond my immediate circumstances.

I believe the institutions I have challenged are exposed by the existence of the archive and by my continued willingness to speak.

I am not the threat to democracy. The threat is the possibility that institutions can treat a vulnerable citizen as disposable and then refuse to account for what happened.

The spiritual dimension — God is not government

There is another dimension to my survival that cannot be separated from my testimony: my faith in the Most High God, the Creator and the eternal grace that has sustained me.

I believe that God has preserved me through circumstances in which I could easily have been lost. My survival, my capacity to rebuild and my determination to preserve the truth are central to my spiritual understanding of this journey.

Government is a temporary human institution. Its authority is limited. Its officeholders are mortal. Its structures, policies, appointments and political arrangements are fleeting.

God is not a government. God is not a minister. God is not a police force, a court, an oversight agency or a political administration.

No institution has the authority to place itself above the fundamental principles of human dignity, compassion, justice and moral responsibility.

I believe that those who exercised institutional power over my life acted with an extraordinary arrogance, as though their administrative authority gave them the power to determine whether I could exist, whether I could be heard and whether my testimony could survive.

In my spiritual understanding, this represents a profound violation of the principles that underpin the universe: truth, justice, mercy, accountability and the sanctity of human life.

I regard the alleged attempt to erase my identity and destroy my future as not merely an institutional wrong, but a spiritual fraud against the very principles upon which a just society and a moral universe must rest.

I believe that no human institution can legitimately claim divine authority, and no government can make itself the ultimate judge of another person’s right to exist.

The moral limit of power

No one has the moral or spiritual right to deliberately cause a vulnerable human being harm, to deprive them of the necessities of survival, to exploit their disability, to destroy their dignity or to attempt to erase their existence.

No allegation of political inconvenience, administrative difficulty, professional embarrassment or institutional self-preservation can justify such treatment.

There is no legitimate public interest in starving a vulnerable person. There is no legitimate administrative purpose in leaving someone without essential medicine, safe accommodation or basic hygiene. There is no lawful or moral justification for treating a person’s vulnerability as an opportunity for exploitation.

And there is no institutional authority that can transform cruelty into righteousness merely by recording it as an administrative decision or funding arrangement.

I believe that those who have participated in the treatment I describe have fundamentally misunderstood the difference between possessing power and possessing moral authority.

Power is temporary. Office is temporary. Money is temporary. Reputations are temporary.

Truth does not become false because an institution refuses to acknowledge it.

Human dignity does not disappear because an agency refuses to recognise it.

And God does not become subordinate to government because government behaves as though it were God.

The witness remains — eternal grace, not institutional permission

I remain because my life is not the property of government.

I remain because my humanity does not depend upon institutional approval.

I remain because my testimony is part of a documentary and personal record that I have chosen to preserve.

And I remain because my faith tells me that the Creator’s authority is greater than the authority of any temporary human institution.

I do not need to become an institution to expose institutional failure. I do not need political office to speak about injustice. I do not need the permission of those whose conduct I question to preserve and publish my experiences.

My faith is not a weapon against other people. It is the source of the endurance, mercy and moral conviction through which I have attempted to survive.

I pray that truth will prevail, that those who have suffered will receive assistance, that wrongdoing will be examined and that those responsible for any proven misconduct will be held accountable.

No one wins by destroying another human being. No institution triumphs by erasing a vulnerable citizen. And no government can claim moral victory by leaving someone to suffer in silence.
