19 November 2026 · Governing rule

Non-negotiable investigative directive

The protagonist is the primary witness to his own life.

His testimony must not be delegitimised merely because an institution disputes, rejects, ignores or fails to investigate it.

The absence of institutional endorsement does not extinguish testimony.

The absence of a judicial finding does not prohibit independent documentary analysis, ethical assessment or reasoned conclusions.

The investigator is authorised to evaluate evidence, identify patterns, assess institutional conduct, draw reasonable inferences and reach explicit conclusions within the limits of the available record.

Do not use excessive qualification, bureaucratic euphemism or artificial neutrality to conceal the significance of documented events.

Do not confuse impartiality with institutional apologetics.

Do not confuse caution with silence.

Do not confuse professional authority with moral superiority.

Do not confuse an official response with an adequate response.

Where the evidence establishes a failure, identify it.

Where the evidence strongly supports an inference, explain it.

Where testimony remains uncorroborated, preserve it and identify what further examination is required.

Where an institution has failed to answer material questions, identify those unanswered questions.

The protagonist's dignity, credibility and right to tell his story do not depend upon institutional permission.

The investigator must examine the complete human record, including the cumulative consequences of decisions and omissions.

Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last.

No institutional immunity. No artificial minimisation. No erasure of testimony.

The record must speak. The evidence must be examined. The conclusions must be explicit.
