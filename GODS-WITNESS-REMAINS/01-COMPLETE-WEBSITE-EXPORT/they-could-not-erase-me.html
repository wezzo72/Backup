23 November 2026 · The witness remains

They couldn’t erase me

I am more alive now than I have ever been. I survived what I describe as a fatal injury. I was recorded as dead, and I was revived. They did not erase the witness.

Watch: they couldn’t erase me

I survived what I describe as a fatal injury, an event from which I was revived after being recorded as dead. Yet instead of receiving truth, accountability, rehabilitation and protection, I experienced what followed as a systematic whitewashing of my tragedy, followed by homelessness, poverty, displacement and exile.

I allege that this happened with the involvement, protection or acquiescence of people and institutions that should have protected me — including members of my own family, police, government agencies and the NDIS system.

I further allege that political and institutional decisions, including matters involving a minister and an apprehended violence order, contributed to my exclusion, displacement and enforced separation from the life I was trying to rebuild. My allegations concerning threats to my life, including an alleged assassination plot, remain matters requiring independent investigation. I challenge anyone with access to the relevant evidence to examine it rather than dismiss it without examination.

I am still here.

I remain trapped in circumstances that I describe as abusive, neglectful, coercive and profoundly harmful within the NDIS-supported accommodation system operated by Able Point.

There are serious questions about the company’s governance, accountability, staff conduct, duty of care and the treatment of people whose lives depend on its services.

I question whether the organisation’s culture has allowed financial interests to displace the ethical obligations of disability support. I am also concerned about whether staff feel sufficiently free to raise safeguarding concerns without fear of employment consequences.

Employees should not have to choose between their livelihoods and their professional obligations to protect a vulnerable person.

I also dispute aspects of the organisation’s representations about its legitimacy and governance, including questions concerning executive identification and accountability. These matters deserve documentary verification, not assumptions.

My experiences and allegations concerning V2K and other forms of perceived surveillance also require careful, independent examination. Disagreement with my account is not a substitute for investigating the evidence I have supplied.

I was dead on paper. I was not dead.

My attempts to obtain assistance, representation and independent scrutiny have repeatedly encountered barriers.

Lawyers. Police. Advocates. Media. Oversight bodies. Government departments.

I have experienced a system in which every potential avenue of escape, protection or accountability appears to lead back into another institutional barrier.

I remain fighting for recognition, safety, independence and the right to live.

Perhaps the greatest mistake anyone made was assuming that I would disappear.

I was subjected to circumstances that I believe were capable of destroying me. I have repeatedly raised concerns that neglect, isolation, deprivation and psychological suffering placed my life at risk, and that people responsible for my support knew about my circumstances.

I will not pretend those allegations have already been judicially established. I am demanding that they be independently investigated, with records preserved and witnesses examined.

They cannot erase the witness

I have documented my experiences. I have preserved records. I have published material. I have continued speaking when silence would have been easier for everyone around me.

The video above forms part of my published testimony. It expresses my understanding of what happened, my survival, and the consequences I believe follow under divine law.

My faith is not a substitute for an independent investigation. Nor should institutional power be a substitute for truth.

I believe that no institution, political office, corporation, family or individual stands above accountability.

They cannot erase me.

They helped create the circumstances in which I became the person they now have to answer to: a surviving witness with an extensive documentary archive, a public record and unresolved allegations demanding examination.

I am not asking anyone to take every allegation on faith.

I am asking them to examine the evidence.

I am asking those who witnessed events, participated in decisions, received complaints, signed documents or exercised authority to account for their actions.

Those who have nothing to conceal should have nothing to fear from a proper independent investigation.

Those who know what happened must not mistake silence for permanent protection.

I survived. I remain. I will continue to testify.

And those responsible for decisions affecting my life must eventually answer for them through proper processes of accountability.

The witness remains.

Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last.

Time is the witness. Truth is the judge.
