EDITORIAL DECLARATION

This second edition expands the original Gospel of the Moral Prison into an integrated documentary,

theological and forensic narrative.

The purpose is to bring together the author’s personal testimony, historical correspondence, official records,

research, prophetic writings and Christian reflections in a continuous account.

This work is not a court judgment, a government investigation, a finding of criminal conduct or an

independently adjudicated account of every allegation.

It is a documentary testament written from the perspective of its author, supported by identifiable materials

preserved in the public archive.

The evidentiary classifications are:

* PRIMARY DOCUMENT: An original document or a copy of an identifiable institutional record.

* PERSONAL TESTIMONY: An account given by the author.

* PROPHETIC ANTECEDENT: An earlier spiritual or prophetic publication.

* ACADEMIC ANALYSIS: A conceptual or research-based examination.

* AUTHOR’S INTERPRETATION: The author’s explanation of the significance of the material.

* UNRESOLVED QUESTION: A matter requiring further independent examination.

Where a document is available only as a repository copy, it is identified as such. Where the issuing agency

has not independently confirmed it, this edition does not claim that authentication has occurred.

The narrative must never conceal contradictory evidence, omit relevant qualifications or convert an

allegation into an established fact.

The reader must be able to move from the words of the Gospel directly to the source material.

THE HUMAN BEING AT THE CENTRE OF THE TESTAMENT

1. The simplest request humanity can receive

Every human being deserves to be treated as a human being.

Page 5

This proposition requires no extraordinary intellectual achievement, no political allegiance, no professional

qualification and no religious conversion. It requires only the recognition that another person’s dignity does

not depend upon their usefulness, productivity, social standing, financial position, disability, reputation or

agreement with the prevailing opinions of others.

PERSONAL TESTIMONY: I have repeatedly described my central request as a request for ordinary

kindness, care, dignity and recognition. I have not asked humanity to worship me. I have asked to be treated

as a person deserving of the ordinary freedoms and protections that other people expect for themselves.

BOOK I

THE MORAL PRISON

CHAPTER 1 — HUMANITY’S INVISIBLE CONFINEMENT

1.1 The prison of conscience

A prison does not always require walls.

A person can occupy a position of considerable authority while being unable to acknowledge the

consequences of their decisions. A professional can possess extensive knowledge while failing to recognise

the human consequences of a particular action. An institution can have elaborate procedures while remaining

unable to explain whether those procedures produced a fair outcome.

This is the philosophical prison examined in this Gospel: the condition in which self-protection, fear,

reputation and institutional loyalty prevent honest moral examination.

BOOK II

THE DOCUMENTARY REVELATION

CHAPTER 4 — THE RECORD AND THE WITNESS

4.1 Why documents matter

Memory is important, but documentary records can provide additional chronological and contextual

information.

A dated letter may establish that a communication was prepared. A decision may establish the recorded

reasoning of an agency. A complaint may establish that a concern was formally expressed. A response may

establish how an organisation dealt with that communication.

The evidentiary value of each document depends on its provenance, completeness, authenticity and context.

4.2 The documentary trap

Page 11

I have used the expression The Documentary Trap to describe the possibility that institutional records,

created for administrative purposes, may eventually become material through which the institution’s own

decisions can be examined.

A record may contain an explanation, an omission, a contradiction or a sequence of decisions that deserves

further investigation.

The document must speak for itself before an interpretation is imposed upon it.

APPENDIX B

FORENSIC PUBLICATION REQUIREMENTS

The following requirements apply to every future revision.

1. Every substantive paragraph must have a relevant primary document, prophetic antecedent, personal

testimony or clearly identified analytical basis.

2. Every official document must be linked directly to its identifiable source copy.

3. Where the original issuing authority’s website is available, it should also be linked.

4. Each document should have its date, author or issuing organisation, document type and relevant reference

number recorded where available.

5. Every quotation must be checked against the original document.

6. No quotation may be reconstructed from memory.

7. No legal conclusion may be attributed to a document without examining its actual language.

8. No criminal allegation may be represented as an established finding without an appropriate determination.

9. No blockchain timestamp may be represented as proof of the truth of a document.
