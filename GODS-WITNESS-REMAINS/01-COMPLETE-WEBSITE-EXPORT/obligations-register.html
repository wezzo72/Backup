Obligations and breach register

Role and purpose. The command is saved. This page is the execution of what could be examined in this pass.

The role, the allegiance, the task, and the final instruction are saved at the command file. The paste began “OLE AND PURPOSE”. This page is the execution: the thirteen outputs, against the documents this pass actually used. It is not a claim that every file in the GitHub archive was opened tonight.

This review does not act for the witness, for any agency, for police, for an ombudsman, for a lawyer, for a media organisation, or for an NDIS provider. A letter proves what that letter says. An allegation remains an allegation until the elements of the duty and the facts are both on the file that was actually read.

1. Executive summary

This pass examined the public pages of this website that already set the law beside named files, above all the law and the files, the Attorney-General letter as reproduced, the humanitarian SOS, Ben’s texts, Able Point, and the compliance record. It did not open every PDF in the GitHub archive. Anything not opened is marked not examined. It is not marked “not a breach”.

Counts, on the issues actually classified below: confirmed breach or contravention, 0. Likely breach, 0. Serious indicator, 8. Policy or ethical failure, 2. Procedural failure, 3. Systemic failure, 1, and that one is the pattern, not a single offence. Unresolved, 7. Not a breach, 1, and only where the document itself shows a response that the statute asked for, not where an office denied the story.

The most serious cluster is not a conviction. It is this: a person with a disability has a public NDIS plan and a published request to leave a provider; the NDIS Act puts a code, a complaints system and a reportable-incident system on a registered provider; the site holds the participant’s notices and the provider’s alleged non-response; those notices have not been matched, in this pass, to a provider incident report or a Commission outcome. Beside that sits a written Commonwealth pattern of referral without investigation, including the Attorney-General’s Department letter of 19 September 2023, a June 2023 notice that a disclosure was not allocated, and an Ombudsman complaint followed by a later service restriction.

Urgent and practical, separate from any finding: food, medicine, a working telephone, and a way out of the address published on the SOS. A statute does not deliver those. The address on the SOS is 55B Archbold Road, Long Jetty. Phone and PayID 0431 300 940.

6. Human-rights assessment

Australia has ratified the treaties below. Ratification does not make the treaty a crime or a domestic cause of action. The articles are an interpretive and accountability standard. They are not a second verdict.

  • Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. Australia ratified it. Articles engaged by the testimony, not yet by a treaty-body finding: 12 (equal recognition), 13 (access to justice), 15 (freedom from degrading treatment), 16 (freedom from exploitation, violence and abuse), 19 (living independently), 22 (privacy), 28 (adequate standard of living). Classification: serious indicator where the SOS and the NDIS notices describe deprivation and a blocked way out. Not a confirmed violation.
  • Convention against Torture. Article 1 is a high threshold: severe pain, intent, and a public-official nexus. The witness uses the word torture for the conditions. That word is his testimony. This pass does not find article 1 established. Article 16, cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment, is the closer question, and it remains a serious indicator, not a finding.
  • International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. Articles 7, 9, 14, 17, 19 and 26 are the ones the testimony reaches: degrading treatment, liberty, a fair hearing and legal aid, privacy, expression, and equal protection. Domestic effect is through interpretation and the Commission, not a self-executing crime. Classification: unresolved on each article.
  • International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights. Articles 11 and 12, adequate living and health, match the SOS as a description of need. They do not, by ratification alone, order a payment.

A communication to a United Nations treaty body is a pathway after domestic remedies are identified. It is not started by this page.

10. Cumulative and systemic analysis

Read together, the documents named on the law page do one thing that no single letter does. They show the same movement more than once: a complaint or a disclosure is received; it is referred, not allocated, restricted, or sent to another office; the next office is not shown, in the pages examined, to have determined the underlying facts. That pattern is a systemic failure of resolution. It is not, on this pass, a proved conspiracy, a proved direction from one minister, or a proved agreement among agencies. Conspiracy needs evidence of agreement. What these letters show without that agreement is fragmentation with a repeated result: no decision on the merits.

The human cumulative effect is the SOS. Hunger, a blocked drain, no telephone, and no way to leave are not cured by classifying them as unresolved questions of criminal liability. They are the present harm against which the duties in BR-01 are measured.

  • NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, for the Code, incidents and complaints. Current. Critical.
  • Commonwealth Ombudsman, knowing a prior restriction may affect how a new complaint is received. The restriction must be read before a new complaint is sent.
  • National Anti-Corruption Commission, only with the existing statement and any new primary document. A second narrative is not new evidence.
  • Australian Human Rights Commission, disability discrimination, if the adjustments and the services are specified against sections 24 and 25.
  • Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security, only for an intelligence agency inside its jurisdiction. A second referral is useless if the first refusal still stands and is not answered.
  • NSW Trustee and Guardian, internal review of the financial arrangements, and the tribunal that made or can review the order. The order has to be identified first.
  • Legal Aid review, under the NSW Act’s own review path, not under section 56.
  • Police: a request for the occurrence that matches the words “close call”, kept separate from a request for the AVO file. The site already has a letter prepared for that. This review did not send it.
  • Administrative review or judicial review only after the exact decision, the Act it was made under, and the time limit are identified. OB-08 is the Commonwealth pathway. It is not a general right to reopen a life.
  • Evidence preservation: keep the original PDFs, the screenshots, and the hashes already published. Do not overwrite them.

13. Final impartial assessment

What is established by text actually reproduced on this website: on 19 September 2023 the Attorney-General’s Department, after a referral from the Prime Minister, told Dr Richard McLean to take an ASIO concern to the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security and other agency concerns to the Commonwealth Ombudsman, and apologised for delay. That letter did not investigate.

What is strongly supported by the way the archive is organised, and not re-proved tonight by re-reading each PDF: there is a set of official notices — a disclosure not allocated, an Ombudsman complaint, a later service restriction, an NDIS plan, a SIL decline, a Trustee section 122 record, a Legal Aid refusal, an LECC out-of-jurisdiction letter — and a set of participant notices to an NDIS provider. Together they are enough to require an investigation. They are not enough, unread, to convict any person.

What remains allegation: coordination, a black budget, an ordered killing, a fabricated sexual report, and personal criminal liability of any named minister, officer, or chief executive. Those allegations are not dismissed. They are not proved by this page.

The witness does not need an institution to grant him a life, a voice, or the right to publish the letters. The institutions do need to answer the letters they wrote. This register is the list of duties those letters engage, and the list of files still to be opened before any duty can be called broken in law.
