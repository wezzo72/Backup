The declaration

I refused to die in the ashes of human betrayal.

But I survived.

The darkness and the rebuilding

I looked directly into my own darkness. And I am not merely the person who survived. I am the person who continued to dream, who found the strength to heal, who rebuilt without the rescue that never came, and who still believes that a better life is possible.

I have known the darkness of betrayal, but I will not make betrayal my identity. I have experienced the consequences of abandonment, but I will not abandon myself. 11. THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK: WHAT THE RECORDS MUST BE TESTED AGAINST

The most relevant legal starting points include: National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 NDIS Code of Conduct NDIS incident management and reportable incidents Disability Discrimination Act 1992 Privacy Act 1988 Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013 Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities The relevant question is not whether a law sounds applicable in the abstract. It is whether the facts satisfy the requirements of the particular provision, whether the person or entity was subject to it, and what remedy or enforcement power is available.

A complaint may raise a serious concern without proving a criminal offence. An administrative decision can be wrong without being corrupt. A report to a regulator is not a finding by that regulator.

Those distinctions matter because the goal is to establish what happened and secure an appropriate response—not to substitute one unsupported conclusion for another.

12. THE HUMAN COST IS NOT A FUCKING FOOTNOTE

I have described the need for stable accommodation, reliable support, safety, dignity and independence. I have described the burden of assembling records, repeatedly seeking answers and trying to navigate organisations whose responsibilities are divided across different people and agencies.

I want those consequences taken seriously.

A service agreement is not a home.

A plan budget is not proof that a service was delivered.

A referral is not the same thing as a resolution.

An incident-management policy is not proof that an incident was managed correctly.

A letter of acknowledgement is not a remedy.

If a support arrangement breaks down, the participant has to live with the consequences. If an accommodation issue remains unresolved, the consequences are practical. If a complaint does not answer the underlying question, the complainant is still left with the problem.

I am asking for a proper assessment of the decisions, the resulting gaps and the remedies available.

That is not a demand for special treatment. It is a demand that the system’s own responsibilities be taken seriously.
