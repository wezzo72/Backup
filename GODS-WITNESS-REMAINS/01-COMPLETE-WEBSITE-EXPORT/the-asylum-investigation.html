3. The legal test, stated before the story

Article 1A(2) of the 1951 Convention, read with the 1967 Protocol, is the test. A refugee is a person who, owing to well-founded fear of being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion, is outside the country of his nationality and is unable or, owing to such fear, unwilling to avail himself of the protection of that country.

Every element is separate. Missing one element means Convention refugee status is not made out, however serious the other elements are.

  • Nationality, or statelessness and former habitual residence.
  • Being outside that country.
  • A well-founded fear, which has a subjective part and an objective part.
  • Persecution, judged in nature, duration, repetition and cumulative effect. Ordinary maladministration is not automatically persecution.
  • A Convention reason: race, religion, nationality, particular social group, or political opinion, including an opinion imputed to the person.
  • A causal link between that reason and the harm.
  • Inability or unwillingness to obtain the protection of the country of nationality. An unsatisfactory reply is not, by itself, proof that protection does not exist.

Article 1C deals with cessation. It applies after status exists. Article 1D concerns people already receiving protection from another United Nations agency such as UNRWA. Article 1E concerns people who already have the rights of nationals in another country. Article 1F excludes people for whom there are serious reasons to consider they committed specified serious crimes. None of those clauses was triggered by a document read in this pass. No conviction of the claimant was identified.

Article 33 prohibits refoulement of a refugee. Human-rights law adds a separate bar. Article 3 of the Convention against Torture prohibits sending a person to a state where there are substantial grounds for believing he would be in danger of being subjected to torture. The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights protects life, liberty, and freedom from cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment. Those instruments can matter inside a country as human-rights complaints. They are not the same thing as recognition as a refugee.

The Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court concerns genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and the crime of aggression, inside the Court’s jurisdiction. The ICC does not grant refugee status. Calling treatment persecution, corruption or abuse does not make it an international crime. This pass did not find the elements of a widespread or systematic attack against a civilian population established by an official record. A statement that a file was sent to the ICC is the claimant’s account of a submission unless a Court record is produced.

4. Australian protection law is a different test, and it does not fit a citizen

Section 36 of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) sets the criteria for a protection visa. The criterion in section 36(2) is that the applicant is a non-citizen in Australia: either a refugee, or a person who would face a real risk of significant harm as a necessary and foreseeable consequence of being removed from Australia. Significant harm is defined to include arbitrary deprivation of life, the death penalty, torture, and cruel or inhuman treatment or punishment.

An Australian protection-visa application and an asylum application made in another country are not the same procedure. Section 36 is for a non-citizen. Dr McLean’s own published account, the Australian addresses on the government letters, and the fact that Australian agencies administer his pension, NDIS supports and financial administration, place him in Australia as an Australian. No passport or citizenship certificate was opened in this pass. Classification of nationality: claimant testimony, strongly consistent with the way Australian offices write to him. Classification of the protection-visa pathway: on that material, section 36 does not open, because the Act requires a non-citizen.

5. Nationality and the territorial element

Country of nationality for the claim he is asking to test: Australia. No second nationality was identified. Statelessness was not identified. The country against which he says he needs protection is the country of his nationality.

On 12 November 2026 the published address of the claimant is 55B Archbold Road, Long Jetty, New South Wales. He is not outside Australia.

Article 1A(2) requires him to be outside the country of nationality. That element is not met. This is not a technical quibble. It is the element that decides Convention refugee status. The same conclusion is already written on the legal-significance page of this site. This investigation agrees with it.

His statement that he is “outside human jurisdiction” is recorded. Classification: claimant testimony and spiritual belief. It is not a geographical fact, and it does not satisfy the Convention.

A person forced from home but still inside his own country is, in UNHCR’s descriptive language, closer to an internally displaced person than to a refugee. That description is not a status the Convention grants, and this report does not confer it. It explains why the refugee label does not yet fit the map.

The one lawful way the territorial element could later be tested is if he were physically outside Australia and asked another state, or UNHCR in that state, to assess a fear of return. This report does not advise him to leave. It does not draft an application that pretends he has already left. Leaving would be his decision, taken with a lawyer, and it would not by itself prove the other elements.

The number he publishes is UR/UST/23/AUS/17. The filename and the page on this site identify it as an Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights reference, not as a UNHCR refugee-status decision. The companion file is the ONHCR asylum-claim PDF.

It is not, by itself, global recognition of refugee status.

Religion

Nationality as a ground of persecution

Membership of a particular social group

Two characteristics are in his testimony and would, in principle, be examined under UNHCR’s social-group guidance: disability, including a stated brain injury, and the fact that he is a gay man. Both can be innate or immutable. That is not enough. The decision-maker must also see a group perceived as set apart, and a link between the group and the harm.

The hospital note records a psychiatric history and disputes his credibility in the clinicians’ words. It does not record a finding that he was harmed because he is gay or because he is disabled. His testimony says both were used against him. Classification: claimant testimony. Assessment: the characteristics are arguable. The nexus is unresolved.

Political opinion

He has made public-interest disclosures and has written to the Prime Minister, the Attorney-General, police, the Ombudsman, IGIS, the NDIS and the Court. Whistleblowing can be political opinion, including opinion imputed by the state. The letters show that offices received political and integrity complaints. They do not say, in the sentences already printed, that they acted in order to punish the opinion. MC23-028244 is a referral, not a reprisal finding.

Assessment: actual political opinion, in the sense of sustained public complaint, is supported by the correspondence. Imputed political opinion as the cause of the harm is arguable and unproven.

State protection

He sought protection. The chronology of asking is the strongest part of the file, and it cuts both ways. He was not ignored into a world with no offices. Offices answered, often by sending him to another office. A referral is a response. A referral to an office that has already closed its door, if that is what the Ombudsman restriction and the IGIS file say when opened side by side, is a weak response. A weak response is not automatically a finding that Australia is unable or unwilling to protect.

Where the alleged actor is the state itself, the protection question changes. You do not ask the persecutor to protect you from himself and then treat the refusal as a separate mystery. That doctrine applies only after persecution by the state is established. It is not established here. It is alleged.

Assessment: failure of a satisfactory remedy is arguable on the referral loop. Absence of state protection in the refugee-law sense is unresolved.

Prospective risk

Refugee status looks forward. Past harm can evidence future risk. It does not replace it.

What is current, on his testimony dated 10 to 12 November 2026, is immediate: food, medicine, a telephone, bedding, plumbing, and a support arrangement he says he cannot leave. That is a present humanitarian emergency. It is published on the SOS. It does not require a refugee finding to be serious.

What is not established is a present official plan to kill him, a black-budget operation, or ongoing electronic attack. Those remain allegations. Ben’s messages, published at Ben’s texts, are third-party testimony about what Ben wrote. They are not a police occurrence.

Internal relocation inside Australia is the practical question while he remains a citizen here: whether he can leave Long Jetty. His testimony is that money, a vehicle and support have been removed. That is a fact-pattern for guardianship review, NDIS review and housing help. It is not the Convention relocation test, which applies when a person is being assessed for protection from a country he is outside of.

Exclusion and cessation

Article 1F: no conviction was identified. He says there has been no charge. That claim was not checked against a national criminal-history extract in this pass. Assessment: exclusion not shown. Not formally cleared.

Articles 1C, 1D and 1E: no prior refugee status, no UNRWA protection, no second nationality. Not engaged.

9. What weakens the claim

These points are kept because hiding them would make the report useless to a lawyer.

  • He is in Australia. The Convention requires him to be outside it.
  • Section 36 of the Migration Act requires a non-citizen.
  • UR/UST/23/AUS/17 is an OHCHR reference in his published file, not a refugee grant.
  • The hospital record that proves the seriousness of 2021 also records clinicians treating his account of systemic injustice as bound up with paranoia and animosity. A lawyer cannot pretend that sentence is not in the note.
  • The assassination, black-budget, V2K and recruitment allegations are his testimony and his filenames. They are not findings of this investigation.
  • Many offices did answer. The answer was often a referral. That supports a complaint about process. It also shows the state was not silent in the total sense.
  • Spiritual declarations of jurisdiction and divine favour are not evidence of a Convention ground.
