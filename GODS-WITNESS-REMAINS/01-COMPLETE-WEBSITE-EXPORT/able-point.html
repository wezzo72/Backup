He formally requests a criminal, financial, regulatory, safeguarding and human-rights investigation into Able Point Australia, including directors, executives, management, employees, contractors and any other person who participated in decisions affecting his accommodation, disability supports, finances, personal safety and rights.

He alleges that his experience raises questions about whether the organisation met its statutory obligations, whether funding was properly claimed, whether support was actually delivered, whether foreseeable risks were addressed, and whether decisions affecting his independence were made lawfully.

He asks that possible offences be investigated, including fraud, obtaining a financial advantage by deception, and false or misleading records, and that possible NDIS breaches, discrimination, financial abuse, neglect and privacy failures also be examined.

He says the complaint is not one missed appointment. It is the cumulative effect of accommodation, support, money, safeguarding and the treatment of a person who depended on the service.

No individual is declared criminally guilty by this complaint. The purpose is to identify suspected conduct, preserve evidence, establish who was responsible, and ask the authorities that have the power to investigate.

2. How the dossier sorts a claim

  • Documented fact: supported by an identifiable record.
  • First-person testimony: his account.
  • Allegation: conduct he says must be investigated.
  • Inference: a possible explanation that still needs corroboration.
  • Legal issue: a provision that may be engaged.
  • Finding: a conclusion only after a proper investigation.

He notes the limit himself: a registered provider must notify the NDIS Commission of abuse or neglect of a participant in connection with the provision of supports within the statutory time. That is not a general duty to report every threat by an unrelated third party. The connection to the supports has to be established. Reportable incidents are explained by the Commission.

AP-03. Money claimed, and whether the support was delivered

He alleges that support payments continued despite failures of attendance and delivery. He states the limit in the same breath: continued payment does not establish fraud unless the claim was improper and the required knowledge and dishonesty are proved.

He asks four records to be compared: NDIS claims, dates, line items and amounts; rosters and attendance; progress notes and daily logs; and his messages, complaints and missed appointments. Each disputed transaction is to be examined on its own. He writes the test as the sum of amounts claimed for services that were not supported.

Provisions he asks to be considered, again as the law to be applied, not as a verdict: NDIS Act sections 59A, 59AC, 59AD and 59AE; NSW Crimes Act 1900, sections 192E, 192F and 192G. He records that the serious contravention of section 59AD is stated in the Act as 10 years or 2,000 penalty units or both, and that section 192E is stated as a maximum of 10 years.
