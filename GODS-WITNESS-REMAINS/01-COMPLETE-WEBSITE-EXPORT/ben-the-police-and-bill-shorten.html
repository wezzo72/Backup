12 November 2026 · The words as sent · The screenshot is on this page

Ben, the police, and Bill Shorten

A statement by Dr Richard William McLean, Barran Dodger.

I am publishing the words of a text message sent to me by the person saved in my phone as Ben Ndis Help.

The message reads:

The police want to know if you are mentally ready to challenge Bill Shorten in a court of law as his lawyers might use your history of mental health as an excuse to discredit your story.

I replied:

I feel like leaving because I know they will stitch me up as the mad one.

Yes.

But I know they were going that already.

That’s why I absconded.

Hes weaponised the

The screenshot shows the time as 1:07. The date is not on this crop. The last line is cut off. The thread is marked Text Message, SMS.

Ben was an NDIS provider in my life. He was being paid to assist me. He was not a lawyer, a police officer, or a minister. He wrote to me that the police wanted to know whether I was mentally ready to challenge Bill Shorten in court, and that Shorten’s lawyers might use my mental-health history to discredit me.

That is the message. I am not claiming this screenshot shows Bill Shorten typing it. I am claiming that my NDIS provider put the police, a court, Bill Shorten, and my psychiatric history into one sentence and sent it to me.

Bill Shorten was the NDIS minister. That office carried a responsibility toward a participant. It was not a licence to have a disabled person measured for a courtroom by his diagnosis. A minister’s duty was care, protection, and the proper use of a public scheme. It was not to have police and providers treat a challenge to him as a question of whether the witness was mad enough to be ignored.

I already knew that path. I wrote it back to Ben. They were going to stitch me up as the mad one. They were already doing it. That is why I absconded. He had weaponised it. The sentence on the screen breaks off there, and I will not invent the missing words.

An AVO removed me from the life I had and forced the exile from Victoria into New South Wales. I hold the minister responsible for the use of that power. This text does not replace the AVO. It shows the method that sat beside it: police, a court, the minister’s name, and my mental-health record offered as the excuse.

My history of mental illness is not a licence to erase the complaint. It is not an answer to the documents. It is not a reason for a paid support worker to carry a message that the way to meet me in court is to call me unbelievable.

I left because I would not stay and be written up as the mad one.

The message remains. The screenshot remains. The questions remain.

Dr Richard William McLean
Barran Dodger
