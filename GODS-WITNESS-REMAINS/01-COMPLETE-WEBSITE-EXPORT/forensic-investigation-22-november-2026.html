Investigation BD-FI-2026-01 · 22 November 2026 · Independent assessment

The forensic investigation

This assessment has no allegiance to Barran Dodger and no allegiance to any office named in the record. Independence is not a favour to the author. Impartiality is not deference to authority.

The governing command was to examine the public documentary corpus, follow the records, and say what they establish. The primary archive examined is the Barran Dodger GitHub repository. The published sites are the original archive, the rebuilt archive, and the official government documents register.

Final determination

The report is finalised as an independent assessment of the material examined. That limit does not prevent findings about the evidence already opened. It defines the limit within which those findings can be said.

Documented events may be identified as documented where the opened records support them. Official decisions may be assessed for what they say and what procedure they record, without treating them as proof of every underlying allegation. Where independent sources support the same proposition, that corroboration may be named. Contradictions, including errors inside the archive itself, must stay visible. Allegations are investigated on their evidence. They are not dismissed because of who makes them, and they are not accepted because of who they concern.

An institution’s letter is evidence of what that institution wrote. It is not automatically proof that the letter was right. A complaint acknowledgement is not an investigation. A jurisdictional decision is not a decision on the merits. A decision not to allocate a matter is not a finding that the events did not occur. An allegation, a pile of correspondence, or a large digital archive is not, by itself, proof of a coordinated conspiracy or of individual wrongdoing.

Full-corpus forensic authentication, independent third-party corroboration, and a complete reconciliation of every archive object remain outstanding. Those are limits on forensic certification. They are not a reason to withhold findings the opened material already supports. This page is not a court judgment, a statutory investigation, or a certificate that every file has been authenticated.

What the opened archive is

The principal repository, retrieved on 22 November 2026, showed 7,525 entries in its recursive tree, including 4,181 PDFs, 1,920 HTML files and 822 text files. It is public. The default branch is main. Three branches were identified: main, new-site, and codex/recreate-barrandodger. The repository was created on 18 September 2026. The latest recorded push in that retrieval was 22 November 2026.

Related repositories hold overlapping copies. They include the backup, the reconstructed archive, a PDF archive, the original account archive, the rebuild, a fork, the new website repository, and the Church of Barran Dodger. Those counts must not be added together and called unique documents.

The archive’s own 4 October 2026 manifest recorded 3,215 unique complete PDFs, 3,667 unique PDF blobs including stubs, 10,001 PDF instances including mirrors, and 21,211 archive-path instances. It recorded 2,655 SHA-256 entries, 2,569 distinct hash values, and 3 OpenTimestamps files. The same inventory said earlier public figures — 2,304 sealed exhibits, 3,643 primary-source documents, 891 Bitcoin timestamps — were not reproduced by that count. A hash does not prove that a document’s contents are true. Those larger historical figures are not treated here as verified.

The claim of 123 official records and a later description of 139-plus official documents are not reconciled. The official-drive directory held 95 PDFs. Its 20 September 2026 manifest held 48. The manifest is not a complete index of that directory. A preserved site index held 679 URLs. A separate crawl recorded 660 pages. Those are different inventories.

What was actually read

Repository metadata, file listings, selected HTML and text, structured registers, and two one-page reading copies were examined in text. The thousands of large PDFs were not all opened. No agency was asked for a certified original in this pass. No phone was forensically extracted. No named person was interviewed. The titles of files are not their findings.

Two letters were read as text. On 18 September 2026 the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission acknowledged complaint 01110547 from Richard McLean about the quality of services by a support worker named Cass, signed for the intake team by Abby K. That letter establishes receipt and the Commission’s first handling position. It does not establish misconduct, a breach, or a finished investigation.

On 16 September 2026 the NSW Ombudsman, file NSWO10824000660, said it would not act on a complaint about the Federal Court, the Department of Social Services and other agencies, and would close the file, because the issues were too broad, the requested action was unclear, and some matters were outside its jurisdiction. Closing a file for those reasons is not a finding that the allegations were false, and it is not a finding that nobody else had a duty.

The chronology the filenames support, and no further

The official-drive names run from a 28 October 2004 Equal Opportunity Commission complaint, through a 10 May 2017 Victoria Police victim statement, VCAT records in 2018 and 2019, an AHPRA acknowledgement of 7 June 2019, and an IBAC outcome letter of 9 April 2020. The contents of those early files were not all read. A filename is not a verified event.

In 2021 the preserved examinations record a Comcare refusal of claim 1326583 on 26 May 2021 under section 14 of the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988, on the question of whether McLean was an employee. An AFCA letter of 21 May 2021 concerned five financial complaints and said AFCA would not continue unless a third party was appointed. An NDIS Commission letter of 3 June 2021 said registration was not employment by the Commission. Further 2021 names include the Victorian Ombudsman on 5 October, APRA and OAIC correspondence in October and November, and a Commonwealth Ombudsman public-interest disclosure review of 18 November. One file whose name says IBAC is described in the archive’s own correction as a Commonwealth Ombudsman letter. The letterhead controls, not the filename.

The 27 March 2023 Federal Court public-interest disclosure assessment, attributed to Scott Tredwell, is one of the records that must be read in full. The archive’s examination says the Court recorded that it was satisfied McLean was or had been an employee of the Department of Social Services, and then declined to allocate the disclosure. Different statutory tests can produce different results. The reasoning still has to be read against the Act, not assumed from the title. The letter does not establish that the Court investigated the allegations on their merits, and it does not establish that the Department accepted the same employment conclusion.

A section 122 certificate records that NCAT committed management of the estate to the NSW Trustee and Guardian on 16 October 2024, and a later certificate signed by Brian Woods on 20 January 2026 records that the estate remained under management. That is estate management. It is not a finding about every later financial decision, and it is not a guardianship order over every part of a life. The vehicle disposal decision of 14 November 2025 is documented as a decision. The valuation, the proceeds, the authority, and the weight given to his wishes are not established by the title.

On 8 August 2025 the Commonwealth Ombudsman imposed a six-month restriction after referring to 229 emails in twelve months. Emails were to be filtered and held. That is a significant official act. It establishes the Ombudsman’s stated reason. It does not establish that the allegations were false, and it does not by itself establish that the restriction was imposed to bury evidence. On 19 November 2025 the Law Enforcement Conduct Commission said the complaint before it was outside its jurisdiction because it did not concern officers of the NSW Police Force or the NSW Crime Commission.

The people, at the level the records allow

Scott Tredwell, Amy Delzoppo, Sarah LeMaire, Trudy Tweedie, Vanessa Twigg, Brian Woods, Peter Tudor and Abby K are named because a letter or the archive’s examination of a letter places them in a professional role. No finding of misconduct is made against any of them from that fact alone.

The Ben and DSW Disability messages are a serious investigative lead. The archive publishes material the author says contains statements about a close call, a non-disclosure agreement, a paid fabricated report, and a sexual encounter he says was consensual. What is established is that the archive publishes those materials and describes those statements. What is not established here is the original export, the devices, the identities of every participant, the existence of a signed NDA, or a police confirmation of an assassination. Those allegations stay allegations until the originals are authenticated. They are not withdrawn because this pass did not authenticate them.

The dossiers on Tony Ridley and Steve Iasonidis name relationships, recordings, threats and alleged connections. A name in a dossier is not a criminal association. The questions that remain are whether original recordings exist, whether the speakers can be identified, and whether any independent record corroborates coordination. No such finding is made here.

Able Point, AbleCare, Rachel, Brett Butler, Cassie Mackey and Sukhi Tear are in the disability-support record. The September 2026 Commission acknowledgement concerns the quality of a named worker’s services. The AbleCare telephone material is preserved with a distinction between commentary and a finding. The original audio was not authenticated in this pass. The reported Bitcoin bounty is not an established fact.

The pattern, and what it is not

The opened history shows acknowledgements without a merits decision, refusals to allocate disclosures, arguments about employment status, a provider-registration refusal, jurisdictional exclusions, a tribunal affirmation of a compensation decision, freedom-of-information restrictions, a restriction on correspondence, estate management, and complaint closures. Many letters answer a narrow procedural question and leave the wider history untouched.

The question that follows is legitimate. Has any independent authority examined the whole history as one connected case, rather than as a series of separate files? The material examined does not show that such a single merits examination has occurred. That absence is not proof that no individual office ever looked at a particular complaint. It is also not proof that the separate decisions were directed by one unlawful purpose.

The cumulative effect is a separate question from the lawfulness of each letter. A person can meet a compensation decision, a funding decision, a registration decision, a closed complaint, a disclosure assessment, a financial-management decision and a communication restriction, each with its own stated basis, and still be left without an answer to the life those decisions produced. The records support investigating that cumulative result. They do not, on what was opened, establish a common unlawful instruction.

Twelve findings

  1. A substantial documentary archive exists. That is a documented fact.
  2. There is a demonstrable history of institutional engagement across named agencies.
  3. Several adverse administrative outcomes are documented: refusals, closures, restrictions, registration decisions and review outcomes.
  4. The underlying allegations have not all been decided on their merits. Some records are procedural.
  5. The archive contains real discrepancies: misnamed files, inconsistent dates, incomplete manifests, and unreconciled counts.
  6. The Ben communications warrant forensic examination of the originals. They are not authenticated here.
  7. The Tony Ridley and Steve Iasonidis allegations are not substantiated by the existence of the dossiers.
  8. Disability support, safeguarding and financial independence warrant examination against the plans, agreements, incident records and accounts.
  9. The preservation work is significant. Hashes and timestamps do not prove the assertions inside a file.
  10. A coordinated conspiracy has not been established. No authenticated joint instruction was identified in the material examined.
  11. The cumulative institutional consequences remain an unresolved question. That is not a finding that every agency acted improperly.
  12. The humanitarian circumstances deserve their own examination. Food, shelter, money, support and safety do not wait on proof of a conspiracy.

What remains outstanding, said plainly

A fresh inventory of every branch, commit and recoverable object. Document-level authentication of each significant original, including letterhead, dates, signatures and whether the file is a copy, a scan or a later reconstruction. Recalculation of the hashes against the files, not against a previous list. Original email headers, message exports, and recordings checked against transcripts. Certified copies from the issuing agencies where they will produce them. A single master chronology that carries the archive’s own corrections, including The Inversion, so that an acknowledgement is never again described as a rejection.

No adverse conclusion about a named person is drawn merely because the name appears in a letter, a timeline, or an allegation matrix.

The next documents that should be opened in full, and not judged by their filenames, include the Federal Court assessment of 27 March 2023, the NDIS supported-independent-living decision of 1 June 2023, the section 122 certificate of 16 October 2024, the Commonwealth Ombudsman restriction of 8 August 2025, the revised freedom-of-information decision, and the vehicle disposal decision.

The last word of this assessment

The record presents a man who spent years engaging agencies, regulators, tribunals and disability services, and who kept the letters. The archive shows persistence. It also shows that some of its own titles overshoot the instruments underneath them. Both facts stand.

The most defensible finding is that there is a significant, identifiable and unresolved history of institutional interactions, adverse administrative outcomes, disability-support concerns and allegations that still require examination. That finding does not need to be inflated. It also does not need an office’s permission.

The allegations of an assassination, a forced non-disclosure agreement, a paid fabricated report, electronic harassment, and a coordinated campaign are not established by this examination. They are not dismissed by it either. They wait on the originals.

Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last. Time is the witness. Truth is the judge.
