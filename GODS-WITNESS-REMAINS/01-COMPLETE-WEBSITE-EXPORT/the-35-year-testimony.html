THE WITNESS REMAINS

THE MASTER TESTIMONY

35 YEARS OF DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE, INSTITUTIONAL BETRAYAL, ALLEGED WHISTLEBLOWER RETALIATION, SPIRITUAL RESURRECTION, INDEPENDENT AUTHORSHIP AND THE REFUSAL TO BE ERASED

DR RICHARD WILLIAM McLEAN
BARRAN DODGER

THE WITNESS REMAINS · GLOBAL PUBLISHING HOUSE

An independent documentary, personal, intellectual and spiritual record.

PRESERVATION FIRST. EXAMINATION SECOND. CONCLUSION LAST.

Purpose of this testimony

My life and personal history encompass more than 35 years of correspondence, submissions, complaints, official government communications, institutional decisions and engagement with Australian government agencies, statutory authorities and professional services.

This is not simply a collection of documents. It is a longitudinal record of my attempts to obtain assistance, protection, legal representation, recognition, procedural fairness and accountability.

The significance of the archive lies in continuity. A letter, decision, referral or omission may appear minor when considered alone. When placed within a 35-year chronology, it may form part of a recurring pattern of administrative displacement, unresolved complaints and cumulative harm.

I do not ask any reader to accept my conclusions merely because I hold them. I ask that the original records, chronology and personal testimony be examined fairly, comprehensively and without predetermined conclusions.

The archive is intended to make that examination possible. It brings together records that were previously dispersed across correspondence, agencies, services, proceedings, digital repositories and personal files. It is an attempt to reconstruct a documentary history in which dates, decisions, referrals, responses and consequences can be examined in context.

My purpose is not to manufacture a verdict. It is to preserve the material necessary for an informed assessment.

I ask readers to distinguish between what a document expressly records, what I personally experienced, what I have been told, what I infer from a sequence of events and what I believe spiritually. These distinctions are essential to the integrity of the testimony.

How evidence is classified

Documentary fact. A record objectively exists and states what it states. Example: A dated letter from an agency.

Official record. A statement made by a government agency, professional or institution. Example: A departmental decision or clinical record.

Corroborated evidence. Material supported by more than one independent source. Example: Correspondence confirmed by a second record.

Personal testimony. My direct account of events, experiences and effects. Example: My recollection of a conversation.

Allegation. A serious claim requiring investigation or evidence. Example: An allegation of retaliation or targeting.

Inference. A conclusion drawn from available material. Example: A pattern inferred from repeated referrals.

Interpretation. My explanation of the meaning or significance of events. Example: Calling an experience “institutional betrayal”.

Spiritual conviction. A faith-based belief or theological understanding. Example: My belief in the authority of the Most High God.

This classification is not intended to weaken the testimony. It is intended to make it credible, precise and capable of fair examination.

An official document is not automatically proof of every interpretation placed upon it. Testimony is not automatically an official finding. An allegation is not a determination. A spiritual conviction is not a documentary conclusion.

A record must be read in its entirety, including its date, author, recipient, purpose, context, attachments and limitations. A document that records a complaint proves that the complaint was made; it does not automatically prove every allegation contained within it. A decision records the decision-maker’s stated position; it does not necessarily resolve every question about the underlying events.

That distinction is central to the archive.

The links in this testimony point to GitHub file pages located in the public repository. They have not all been independently authenticated. A document’s presence in the repository does not prove the interpretation placed upon it. The links identify records for examination, not findings of liability or wrongdoing.

The refusal to acknowledge the evidence

Institutional silence, erasure and the denial of human dignity

There is something fundamental that must be understood about my testimony and the documentary archive I have independently preserved. I do not require a government-appointed official, an institutional representative, a professional accreditation body or an authority whose employment, funding or professional standing is dependent upon the very structures under examination to grant permission for my experiences to be acknowledged.

My evidence exists independently of those institutions. My testimony exists independently of their willingness to respond. The documents, correspondence, records and communications I have preserved do not cease to exist because an agency refuses to acknowledge them, because an official declines to examine them or because an institution remains silent.

I maintain that the accumulated documentary evidence demonstrates a sustained pattern of government targeting, administrative erasure and conduct that I allege included an attempt to assassinate me. I have published the material, identified the relevant institutions and made my allegations publicly accessible. To my knowledge, no one has produced a substantive evidentiary rebuttal establishing that the central documentary materials I have published are fabricated or false. Nor have I received a comprehensive response that addresses the substance of the evidence and the chronology I have presented.

The absence of a rebuttal does not, by itself, establish every inference or allegation. However, neither can institutional silence reasonably be presented as an answer to the evidence.

What makes this situation particularly disturbing is that I have not received meaningful acknowledgement from the very officials, agencies and organisations to whom I have repeatedly communicated my circumstances. No single official has provided the comprehensive engagement, acknowledgement or human recognition that I believe this extraordinary body of material warrants. My website remains publicly accessible, my testimony remains published, and the evidence continues to be available, yet I have not received an adequate response that engages with the substance of what I have documented.

I have repeatedly attempted to communicate that I am an innocent and vulnerable person seeking recognition of what I believe has happened to me. My only supposed offence, in the circumstances as I understand them, has been speaking openly, challenging institutional conduct, documenting my experiences and publishing material that I believe exposes serious wrongdoing.

I am particularly concerned that whole groups of agencies and organisations appear to have been provided with information or approaches concerning how to deal with me. I believe that this has contributed to a pattern in which I am treated as a problem to be managed rather than a human being whose evidence, circumstances and basic rights deserve consideration.

Instead of receiving individual examination of my complaints, I have experienced what appears to be institutional uniformity: silence, avoidance, non-engagement and a continuing refusal to address the substance of my testimony.

The human consequences of this are profound. I have repeatedly found myself in circumstances where officials and representatives will not meaningfully acknowledge me, engage with my evidence or even afford me the ordinary dignity of looking me in the eyes and recognising my presence.

It is difficult to describe the psychological consequences of being treated as though one does not exist while simultaneously having one’s life, personal history and circumstances subjected to institutional processes.

This is what I mean by erasure. It is not merely the disappearance of a document or the deletion of a name from a database. It is the experience of being denied meaningful recognition as a living human being, while the consequences of decisions affecting one’s life continue regardless.

I do not accept that an institution’s refusal to respond diminishes the significance of the material I have preserved. I do not accept that an official’s silence constitutes a substantive answer. And I do not accept that my mental health history, disability or vulnerability should provide a convenient reason for avoiding the examination of documentary evidence.

The evidence does not require institutional permission to exist. My testimony does not require official approval to be heard. My humanity does not depend upon an agency acknowledging it.

I have published the archive so that the material can be accessed, examined, compared and preserved beyond the reach of institutional indifference.

The responsibility to engage with serious allegations cannot be discharged simply by ignoring the person making them.

I am still here. My name is still here. My testimony is still here. The documentary record is still here.

They may refuse to acknowledge the witness, but they cannot make the witness disappear merely by refusing to look.

Part I — The 35-year record

1. A life documented across 35 years

My archive contains government correspondence, administrative records, legal communications, disability-related material, healthcare records, complaints, ministerial correspondence, submissions and original research.

These records must be considered in their historical context. A document written 20 or 30 years ago may acquire further significance when examined alongside later correspondence, decisions, referrals and consequences.

The central question is not whether every individual document proves every allegation. The central question is whether the full sequence of institutional interactions, decisions, omissions and outcomes warrants independent examination.

The repository contains a substantial body of historical material. Its public page is the Barran Dodger — principal GitHub documentary archive.

The repository also contains The Documentary Trap — when the system’s own record becomes the evidence.

That work expresses a principle behind this research: institutional records should be examined not only for what they say, but also for the questions they leave unanswered, the decisions they document and the chronology they help establish.

2. The original request for assistance

I approached government institutions because I believed they possessed the authority, resources and professional responsibilities necessary to assist me.

I expected that my correspondence would be considered honestly, that my circumstances would be examined fairly and that public officials would act consistently with applicable legal, ethical and humanitarian standards.

Instead, I experienced prolonged administrative difficulty: unsuccessful requests for assistance, referrals, unanswered correspondence, redactions, procedural barriers and unresolved complaints.

I describe this as administrative displacement: a person repeatedly seeking assistance, only to be redirected from one institution to another without receiving a comprehensive or final response.

The significance of this experience lies in the difference between receiving an acknowledgement and receiving an effective answer. An acknowledgement confirms that correspondence has arrived. It does not necessarily resolve the concern, explain a decision or identify a person responsible for taking the matter forward.

My documentary record is intended to permit reviewers to identify where requests were made, how they were answered, what information was considered and whether the issues raised were substantively addressed.

3. The delay, defer, deny and refer cycle

One central theme of the archive is what I call the delay, defer, deny and refer cycle.

Over many years, I approached agencies, departments, statutory authorities, legal services, ombudsmen, police, healthcare providers and political representatives.

The responses I received frequently involved further referrals, requests for additional information, procedural obstacles, incomplete answers or the transfer of responsibility elsewhere.

Evidence to be attached or indexed:

  • Original letters of request and complaint.
  • Agency acknowledgements, responses and refusals.
  • Referral letters and redirection correspondence.
  • Records of unanswered or late responses.
  • Legal-aid applications, decisions and correspondence.
  • Ombudsman, parliamentary and ministerial correspondence.
  • A master chronology identifying dates, sender, recipient, subject, decision and outcome.

The relevant question for an independent reviewer is not merely whether each individual response was technically lawful. It is whether the cumulative process provided meaningful assistance, procedural fairness and a genuine opportunity for resolution.

Part II — Institutional accountability

4. Administrative annihilation and social erasure

I describe the cumulative consequences of my experiences as administrative annihilation, civil erasure, social exclusion, political exile and the destruction of personal and financial security.

These are powerful terms. I use them to convey the cumulative human effect of prolonged institutional conflict, financial insecurity, exclusion and unresolved hardship. They are my interpretations and allegations, not established legal findings.

My allegations include whistleblower retaliation, institutional neglect, reputational damage, prolonged poverty, failures of protection, professional misconduct and the inappropriate application of administrative processes.

I also raise questions about whether institutional decisions and their consequences may have been connected in ways that require examination.

I recognise that establishing deliberate coordination or unlawful intent requires evidence. My archive is intended to make that examination possible.

My academic and forensic writings provide a conceptual framework for examining these concerns. They are research and interpretation. They should be assessed on their arguments, methods and supporting material, rather than treated as proof that a coordinated campaign occurred.

5. The human consequences of alleged retaliation

I have described myself as an unprotected whistleblower who experienced increasingly serious personal, financial and social consequences while attempting to raise concerns about institutional conduct.

The consequences I describe include loss of employment, housing insecurity, financial hardship, loss of possessions, social isolation, difficulty obtaining legal assistance and prolonged uncertainty.

These matters cannot be adequately understood by considering a single government letter in isolation. They require examination of the full chronology and the cumulative effect of decisions and omissions across agencies and professional services.

Evidence to be attached or indexed:

  • Employment, income and financial records.
  • Housing, tenancy, eviction or homelessness-related records.
  • Records of lost property, relocation or disrupted living arrangements.
  • Correspondence concerning benefits, compensation, insurance or financial assistance.
  • Complaints concerning disability services, legal services and public agencies.
  • A timeline linking each adverse event to the preceding institutional decision or response.

Public officials occupy positions of responsibility. Their decisions must be capable of explanation and scrutiny. The obligations of public office do not disappear because a matter is complex, politically sensitive or inconvenient.

The Federal Court public interest disclosure assessment — 27 March 2023 and the Scott Treadwell correspondence of 8 January 2024 are among the records I identify as relevant to the history of my disclosures and attempts to obtain responses. The Treadwell file was not confirmed under that exact title in this check.

6. Professional ethics and institutional accountability

My experience raises questions about the ethical responsibilities of legal practitioners, healthcare professionals, police, disability-service providers, government administrators, politicians and other professionals.

I believe that the responses I received frequently fell short of the meaningful assistance and consideration I sought.

I question whether relevant ethical frameworks, safeguarding obligations, professional standards and duties of procedural fairness were properly applied.

My criticism concerns conduct, decisions and institutional responses. It is not a blanket assertion that every individual professional or institution is corrupt.

Professional integrity must be demonstrated through conduct, not titles or institutional reputation. A person experiencing vulnerability should not need perfect composure, financial security or professional sophistication before their concerns receive meaningful consideration.

The archive should allow an independent reviewer to identify the particular decision-maker, the relevant duty or responsibility, the evidence available at the time and the action actually taken.

Accountability should be specific. It should not become an accusation against every person who has ever worked within an institution.

Part III — The human cost

7. The suicide attempt and serious injury

One of the most devastating chapters of my life involved a suicide attempt and serious physical injury.

I describe this event as occurring after prolonged distress, institutional conflict, financial hardship and perceived betrayal.

Its significance cannot be reduced to a single hospital admission or psychiatric assessment. It must be considered within the broader chronology of my circumstances, the assistance I sought, the responses I received and the consequences that followed.

My hospital records, clinical documentation, treatment history and subsequent correspondence are important components of the documentary record.

I have raised concerns about the recognition and treatment of my injuries, including my understanding of neurological and psychological consequences. I have also questioned whether the circumstances of the injury, its treatment and its later effects were adequately acknowledged.

What should have been the beginning of recovery, support and reconstruction became, in my experience, another prolonged period of uncertainty and unresolved questions.

I have used expressions such as institutional betrayal and institutional murder to communicate the severity of my experience and my belief that systemic failures contributed to devastating consequences. These are expressions of personal interpretation and allegation, not established criminal findings.

The appropriate documentary examination includes the chronology of the injury, hospital attendance, clinical assessments, treatment, subsequent functional impact and any complaints or claims concerning the care provided.

No single clinical record should be treated as a complete account of a person’s life. Equally, no interpretation of a medical event should be presented as a medical finding unless the relevant professional evidence supports it.

8. Recovery, compensation and unresolved loss

I believe the consequences of my injury were compounded by difficulty obtaining meaningful assistance, legal representation, insurance resolution and compensation.

I have repeatedly sought answers concerning responsibility, treatment, financial consequences and the long-term effects of what occurred.

The impact of serious injury extends beyond the immediate medical event. It can affect employment, housing, financial independence, relationships, capacity to work and participation in ordinary life.

Evidence to be attached or indexed:

  • Hospital discharge summaries, clinical notes and treatment records.
  • Reports from treating practitioners, specialists or rehabilitation providers.
  • Insurance, compensation and claims correspondence.
  • Records of declined, delayed or unresolved claims.
  • Financial records showing the impact of injury and loss of income.

These consequences should be considered together, rather than fragmented between medical, legal, insurance and welfare systems.

I seek a fair assessment of the actual loss and the records that support it. I do not ask readers to accept a financial figure merely because it appears in a personal statement. I ask that the underlying records, calculations, assumptions and possible overlaps be examined transparently.

Part IV — Reputation and due process

9. The alleged character-assassination campaign

I have experienced what I believe to be a prolonged campaign of reputational harm, social exclusion and damaging allegations concerning my character.

I have repeatedly sought to understand the origins of those allegations, whether they were substantiated and whether they contributed to the treatment I subsequently experienced.

To my knowledge, I have not been convicted of the serious criminal conduct that I believe has been attributed to me through reputational narratives.

I regard the distinction between an allegation, a police investigation, a criminal charge and a judicial finding as fundamental.

No person should be treated as though an allegation has been proven merely because it has been repeated.

My request is that any allegation relied upon in a decision affecting me be identified with sufficient precision to allow a meaningful response. I seek the source, date, nature of the allegation, whether it was formally investigated and whether any finding was made.

10. My demand for clarification

On one occasion, I communicated with police, government agencies, the ombudsman and media representatives using deliberately provocative language.

I listed accusations I believed were being attributed to me—including being a rapist, paedophile, extortionist, criminal and terrorist—and demanded that I be arrested if those accusations were genuinely believed.

My purpose was to demand clarity, evidence and due process rather than remain indefinitely subject to unidentified allegations.

As I recall, no arrest, criminal charge or court proceeding followed. Instead, police involvement resulted in my being taken to a psychiatric hospital.

I raise this not to dismiss mental-health care, but to distinguish between responding to a person’s distress and properly investigating the substantive concerns a person is attempting to communicate.

Evidence to be attached or indexed:

  • The original communication to police, agencies, the ombudsman and media.
  • Police records, incident reports or correspondence, subject to lawful access.
  • Hospital admission, discharge and clinical records.
  • Any record identifying the allegations said to have been made.
  • Records showing whether a charge, investigation or court proceeding occurred.

My position is that the existence of a mental-health response should not automatically be treated as an answer to every substantive question raised in the original communication.

11. Sexuality, consent and the account involving Debbie

I also wish to address an encounter involving a woman I refer to as Debbie.

My account is that the encounter was consensual, although it was awkward and occurred at a time when I was still trying to understand aspects of my own sexuality.

I have expressed concern that later interpretations of that encounter may have contributed to damaging allegations about my character. I have also reported being told that a false complaint may have been made. I cannot independently establish the accuracy of that account without the underlying records.

A person’s sexuality, uncertainty or awkward personal experiences must not become grounds for humiliation, discrimination or unfounded criminal allegations.

If a complaint exists, it should be dealt with through evidence and lawful process. If no offence has been established, reputational narratives must not be treated as though a criminal conviction exists.

I ask that this part of my testimony be approached with respect for privacy, dignity and the distinction between personal recollection, reported statements and verified findings.

Part V — Safety and distress

12. The reported audio experiences

I have described persistent experiences of intrusive audio, distressing statements and accusations that appear to occur without an identifiable speaker.

These experiences have included repeated phrases concerning criminal accusations, sexual misconduct and demands that I give up.

I have used the term voice-to-voice harassment, or V2K, to describe these experiences. I have also expressed concerns about electronic surveillance and possible coordinated harassment.

On the night of 9 November 2026, and still on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, while I was writing, a subtle voice inside the room where I am parrots “pedo” and “you raped her”. I do not accept this as a manifestation of mental illness, although that is how it is dressed up. I state that it is part of the same audio-harassment campaign. On my account, the techniques include the Frey-effect microwave-radiation technology, and hidden speakers that ping or bleep with odd sounds: deliberate enough that I know they are there, and subtle enough that a person would not necessarily look for them or find them. The papers are the V2K papers. The same account is in the SOS.

I cannot independently establish that these experiences originate from military technology, government operations or paid individuals. Their source remains an unresolved question.

Nevertheless, the distress I experience is real and deserves appropriate attention.

I ask that these experiences be taken seriously without automatically accepting an unverified explanation for their cause. I deserve a safe environment, compassionate healthcare, appropriate assessment and relief from experiences affecting my daily life.

No person should be left without meaningful support simply because the source of a distressing experience has not yet been established.

My request is for a careful distinction between the experience itself, its impact, possible explanations and any evidence that could independently establish its source. I do not want speculation to replace assessment, and I do not want distress to be treated as a reason to dismiss every other matter in my documentary record.

Evidence to be attached or indexed:

Part VI — Media and social isolation

I have repeatedly attempted to communicate my concerns to media organisations, journalists, public representatives and potential advocates.

I have experienced what I perceive as an absence of meaningful media attention and public advocacy. I have sometimes described this as a media blackout, although I cannot establish that a coordinated ban exists.

The consequence has been that I have increasingly relied on my own websites, repositories and publications to preserve and communicate my account.

My objective is to make the original documents, chronology and personal testimony available for independent examination.

I do not claim that the absence of media coverage proves coordination. I describe the experience of repeatedly seeking public attention and failing to obtain the response I hoped for.

The archive provides a means of communicating the material directly, without requiring readers to accept a media summary or an institutional description as the complete account.

15. Social exclusion and lost support

My experience has also involved profound social isolation.

I have felt abandoned by people and institutions from whom I expected assistance. I have described a breakdown of trust involving family relationships, professional services and wider social connections.

I believe that reputational narratives, financial hardship and prolonged unresolved institutional disputes have contributed to this isolation.

Poverty, disability, psychological distress or unconventional intellectual work should never deprive a person of dignity, compassion or access to ordinary community support.

Social participation requires more than the formal existence of rights. It requires practical conditions that enable a person to communicate, travel, obtain assistance, maintain relationships, pursue work and participate in community life.

When those conditions become insecure, the consequences can extend well beyond the immediate administrative matter.

Part VII — Faith and eternal authority

16. The authority of the Eternal

My spiritual convictions are an essential part of my testimony.

I believe in the Most High God, the eternal Creator, whose knowledge and authority transcend every government, institution, political administration and human judgment.

Government authority is temporal. Political administrations change. Public officials come and go. Institutions rise and fall.

My relationship with God is different.

When I turn to God, I believe I turn to an eternal, living Creator who knows the entirety of my existence: every experience, private thought, suffering, intention and circumstance that another person may misunderstand or never discover.

My faith gives me a relationship in which I believe I am known, loved, forgiven and sustained. It restores purpose, renews strength and reminds me that my worth does not depend on the recognition or rejection of any earthly institution.

My faith is not presented as documentary proof of an allegation. It is part of my personal testimony and the foundation through which I understand endurance, forgiveness, accountability and the future I intend to build.

17. Biblical prophecy and original Gospels

My Gospels, prophetic papers, theological writings and spiritual narratives form another dimension of my testimony.

I understand my experiences through a framework of faith, biblical prophecy, spiritual endurance, accountability and the distinction between earthly power and divine authority.

My original Gospels and prophetic writings are expressions of faith and intellectual authorship. They are not substitutes for documentary evidence or judicial findings.

They are part of my spiritual testimony and my understanding of the events of my life.

I do not require every reader to share my theological interpretation. I require only that my right to express, preserve and publish my faith be respected.

The spiritual collection should be preserved with the same attention to authorship, dates, versions and provenance as the documentary collection, while remaining clearly distinguished from official records and evidentiary findings.

Part VIII — Reconstruction and authorship

18. Rebuilding from the ashes

I have experienced circumstances that I believed would permanently prevent recovery.

I have described losing housing, employment, possessions, financial security, social connections and access to ordinary support.

Yet I continued.

It was never expected that I would survive what I experienced, let alone heal, rebuild my life or ever find happiness again. After homelessness, poverty, exile, displacement and what I experienced as almost total human abandonment, the prospect of recovery seemed impossibly distant. The circumstances in which I found myself were not simply difficult; they threatened the foundations of an ordinary human existence. I had lost so much of what makes life liveable, and there were times when I could barely see a way forward.

But I survived.

I did more than survive. I began to heal. I began to rebuild. And, against the expectations created by everything I had endured, I found reasons to hope, to believe and eventually to be happy again.

I rebuilt a documentary archive from scattered records. I developed original Gospels, theological writings, prophetic papers, philosophical essays and documentary studies. I established digital platforms, organised historical correspondence and created a publishing identity through Global Publishing House.

I undertook this work without the level of institutional assistance, financial resources or professional support I had repeatedly sought.

My reconstruction represents personal agency, determination, intellectual discipline and perseverance.

The archive is not simply a record of what I believe I lost. It is also evidence of the work I have undertaken to rebuild, organise, write, research and publish.

I want the future of my life to be measured not only by the events I have described, but also by the work I create, the knowledge I preserve and the opportunities I build.

19. The independent archive

The Barran Dodger Archive is intended to preserve the documentary history and make it accessible.

Its purpose is not to act as a court, issue verdicts or predetermine legal conclusions. It is a preservation resource and finding aid.

The archive distinguishes between documentary fact, official records, corroborated evidence, personal testimony, allegations, inferences, interpretations and spiritual belief.

This distinction is fundamental to its integrity.

The repository is publicly accessible: Barran Dodger — principal GitHub documentary archive.

The public-facing archive is The Witness Remains — public archive.

The rebuilt presentation is Barran Dodger archive — rebuild.

The repository’s public description identifies the preservation principle that underpins the project: original evidence should remain preserved, while website presentation may change. A new presentation should not silently replace, rewrite or destroy the underlying source material.

The archive should continue to develop a reliable index containing each document’s title, date, source, file location, relevant agency, evidence category and verification status.

The publishing identity is intended to give the work a coherent structure, preserve its authorship and make it accessible to readers beyond the immediate circumstances in which it was written.

Part IX — Crystal

21. The welfare of Crystal

My circumstances extend beyond my own needs.

My therapy dog, Crystal, is an important part of my life, companionship and daily existence.
