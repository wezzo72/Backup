Instalment 2. 17 November 2026. Continues Instalment 1. The video is the architecture. The letters that were opened are the evidence. This is not a verdict.

God Is Furious — the Holy Reckoning

The second part begins where the first stopped. The video’s numbers six to fourteen. The same voice. The same limit. A theme is answered only with a record that was opened. Where the record is the author’s own page, it is called his account. Where it is an office’s letter, it is called the office’s letter.

The video remains Chosen One: GOD Is FURIOUS, The Attraction Code, published 16 November 2026 at 19:07 GMT, 17 November 2026 in Sydney. The reading copy is the transcript. The command is the integrated command.

Number six. One person made to carry what others would not face

At about thirty-six minutes the video says they tried to sacrifice the listener for their own comfort. The image is a village dumping its trash at one door and calling the pile a cleansing. The line it wants remembered is that cruelty dressed as cleansing is still cruelty. It then says a blood debt is due. That last sentence is the video’s theology. It is not a sum this examination can calculate, and it is not a sentence passed on any named person.

What the opened pages show is a man describing himself as the place where other people’s trouble was put, and offices answering him by moving the trouble somewhere else.

On a letter already published on this site he writes that he believes he has become a scapegoat, and that he has been labelled mentally ill in a way that pushes aside the events he wants examined. That sentence is his. It is on the paper that asks for whistleblower status. It is not an office agreeing with the word.

The office letter that was opened, MC23-028244, does a different and narrower thing. A Riley, on 19 September 2023, takes an email that had gone to the Prime Minister on 5 July 2023 and sends the writer to the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security and to the Commonwealth Ombudsman. The letter apologises for delay. It does not take the complaint up. A referral can be a lawful next step. Read against the video’s image, it is also the moment the village points at another door. The complaint does not get a decision. It gets an address.

The same shape appears in the disability file as the plan page states it, and only as that page states it, because the PDFs were not re-opened. An occupational therapy report of 29 February 2024, Georgia Gray, Allied Health 2U, is said to recommend full-time support and Specialist Disability Accommodation with no other participants. An NDIA letter of 1 June 2023 is said to refuse Supported Independent Living and Specialist Disability Accommodation. A plan approval of 20 November 2025 is said to record $366,406.69. Three papers. A recommendation, a refusal, and a later sum. None of them, in the text opened, says the person was sacrificed. Together they say the support he was told he needed and the support an agency agreed to fund are not the same document.

The Able Point page, opened for this instalment, is the complaint he sent on 30 October 2026. It asks police, the NDIS Commission and the NDIA to investigate alleged neglect, alleged financial misconduct, and alleged safeguarding failure. The page says the original service agreements, invoices, rosters and incident reports were not independently inspected. This instalment does not inspect them either. The complaint is a demand. It is not a finding that Able Point committed those acts. A provider who is asked to answer is not, by the asking, guilty.

The human end of this number is not the metaphor of a debt. It is the appeal on the humanitarian SOS. He asks for food, a safe place for himself and for Crystal, a telephone, and someone who will act. The page says an archive cannot feed him, a blockchain cannot treat a dog, and a website cannot be a house. Crystal is described there as the companion who must not be left out of the appeal. Those sentences are his account of the present. No agency assessment of that present was opened. The video’s “blood debt” does not feed either of them. The unanswered practical question is whether anyone with a duty has answered the appeal with food, a door, and a named person. In the pages opened, that answer is not there.

Number seven. A name used, and a payment offered for an ending

At about thirty-nine minutes the video says they cashed in on his ruin and that the universe is demanding a refund. The image is stolen jewellery pawned until the owner arrives with receipts. A reputation built on another person’s destruction is called a house of cards.

Two opened records touch a name being used. Neither is a receipt of a pawnshop.

The first is the bank. CF-15811059C, Reece, 2 November 2026, says NetBank was removed on 15 October 2026 outside the financial-management process and calls it a once-off staff error. Access was restored on 28 October 2026. The bank offers $500, to be accepted by 9 November 2026, tied, on the page’s reading of the letter, to the complaint being treated as resolved and to a release of further claims. The customer’s page says he did not accept. The offer is the office’s. The claim that the money was a substitute for looking at the transfers he asked about is his. The earlier ABN complaints, CF-14935306C and CF-14993212C, are closed unless new information is provided. A payment that ends a complaint is not proof the complaint was empty. It is proof the bank preferred a sum and a release to a further investigation of those closed items.

The second is the set of messages on Ben’s messages. The page identifies pages 54, 64 and 65 of a 123-page file. It prints: “Yes even the police said it was a close call.” “The agencies went back through phone records and believed she was paid to discredit you.” “They made me sign a non disclosure agreement.” “I thought you were just paranoid. You were right.” The page says these are what the author says he received. It says they are not, by themselves, a police finding or proof that any named person ordered a killing. An SMS on the SOS page, contact name Ben Ndis Help, with no date on the screen, is quoted as saying he was being protected better than the prime minister after “the highest level corruption” was blown open. That sentence is a reported statement. It is not a protection order, and it is not a finding about a minister.

If those messages are authentic, they are a third party saying a reputation was attacked and that he was told not to speak. Authenticity was not tested in this instalment. The images were not re-opened as files. No non-disclosure agreement was opened. No phone record was opened. The video’s refund has not been issued by any office in the papers read. What has been issued is the publication of the sentences, which is not the same as the sentences being true.

Number eight. The file was not undecided. It was unanswered

At about forty-two minutes the video says the universe was never undecided. It was letting the scales load. A judge stays quiet so a liar can contradict himself. Patience is called strategy, not doubt.

No opened letter shows an office waiting in order to trap its own writer. That purpose is not in the documents. What is in the documents is a sequence that changes its own story without ever deciding the person’s complaint.

On 14 April 2022 Petra Gartmann, for the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, refused FOI/2022/045. The request of 7 February 2022 was “I request all information government agencies hold on me please.” The decision says the documents cannot be found or do not exist, and that the department does not hold other agencies’ records. On 8 October 2024 the revised decision, FOI/2022/045IC, says the department had told the Information Commissioner there was not enough information for a positive match between the applicant and some material it had found, and the page says five documents were then released. A review was later closed under section 54W(c). The reasons for that closure are named as not being in the reading copy.

That is not a scale that was always sure. It is an office that first said the papers were not there, then said a match could not be made, then released five documents. The contradiction is in the department’s own successive letters. It does not prove the first letter was a lie. It proves the first letter was not the last word, and that the last word still was not an examination of the life the request asked to see. Five documents are not “all information government agencies hold.”

The Attorney-General’s letter sits in the same quiet. It apologises for delay and then does not decide. Delay is documented. A strategy behind the delay is not.

Number nine. They were not shown to be fighting a future. They were shown not to answer the present

At about forty-five minutes the video says the attack was not on who the listener was, but on who he was becoming. Workers tearing down a building before anyone sees its height. The rise itself is called the rebuttal.

This instalment did not open the catalogues, the parliamentary record, the SANE Australia record, or the Victoria University enrolment that the command lists. Those achievements are not confirmed here and they are not denied. They are not yet read.

What is read is the thing he has already become in public: a person who kept the letters and published them. The FOI file, the bank outcome, the referral, the complaint to Able Point, and the SOS are on this site because he put them there. That is a documented act of publication. It is not a prophecy that a platform will vindicate him. The video says the future they feared has arrived. The papers say a record exists and has not been answered in the letters opened. Those are different sentences. The second one can be stood on.

Number ten. Restraint, as a letter that asks for a record and does not ask for a verdict

At about forty-eight minutes the video says restraint was mistaken for weakness, and that mercy was a countdown. A lion in the grass. The guilty confuse delay with denial.

The letter that matches this theme, without the lion, is the letter to police of 3 November 2026. It is addressed to The Entrance Police Station and, because the account mentions Federal Police, to the Australian Federal Police. It says it is not asking the officer to find that an assassination was attempted, that any named person ordered one, or that a provider or a guardian committed an offence. It asks whether records are held, and it says an absence should be written down. It says the letter had not been lodged by the page.

That is restraint in the ordinary sense. He published the accusation and, in the same letter, refused to ask the police to adopt it as a finding. The video would call this mercy that has now expired. The letter does not say the mercy has expired. It says the records should be identified. Whether the letter was received is not shown. A letter written and not shown to have been sent is a text. It is not yet a police file.

His own earlier message to his mother, printed on the Ben page, is not restraint of that kind. It says they will kill him by omission and the violence of neglect, and that a hospital freedom-of-information file deemed something a lethal attempt. The hospital file, reference IR8415987, was named on the plan page and was not opened. The iMessage is his fear, dated by him as before the assassination account was written up. It is not the hospital record.

Number eleven. Delay was real. A death warrant was not written

At about fifty-one minutes the video says they mocked justice as absent until the delay became their death warrant. A storm on the horizon. The arrogant say nothing is coming. The first strike is a roar. Every extra insult is another brick in the weight.

The dates that were actually read can be set in a line.

That line is a delay. It is years between an ask and a letter that does not decide the ask. The video calls the end of the delay a death warrant for the people who waited it out. No paper opened here is a warrant, a sentence, or a finding that any person will fall. The Holy Reckoning, in the command’s own definition, is accountability without revenge. The line of dates is the accountability question. It is not the revenge.

Number twelve. The quiet is the file

At about fifty-four minutes the video says silence was not surrender. It was the evidence that sealed their sentence. One side screams. The other sits still. The judge treats the screaming as the exhibit.

The still thing, in this record, is the paper. He did not need this examination to invent a story. The department wrote the refusal. The department later wrote the release. The Attorney-General’s Department wrote the referral. The bank wrote the staff error and the offer. He wrote the SOS and the police letter and the Able Point complaint. Ben’s page prints what he says Ben wrote. Each of those is an exhibit of a different kind. An office exhibit shows what the office said. His exhibit shows what he asked. A quoted message shows what a page says a message said. Putting them in one narrative does not melt them into one proof.

The video says the verdict is already final. It is not. The questions the papers leave open are still open. Was the staff error only a staff error? Do the five released documents match the man who asked? Did police ever record a close call? Was an agreement signed? Did the support that was funded arrive as food, a door, and a person at the address on the SOS? Those questions are the unfinished case. Silence did not close it. Silence is why it can still be read.

Number thirteen. A name the office said it could not find, and the name on the letterhead anyway

At about fifty-seven minutes the video says they tried to erase him and the universe is writing the name in gold. Spray paint on a mural. The artist returns and makes the damage part of the picture. They do not get to edit the next chapter.

Gold is the video’s word. The documentary word is simpler, and it is the inversion the command asked for.

He does not need an office to invent a history for him. The history he is asking them to account for is the history on their own pages. FOI/2022/045 said the documents could not be found or did not exist. The letter that said so is itself a document, addressed to Dr Richard McLean, dated, signed Petra Gartmann. It was the addressee of the claim that the rest of the file was empty.

That does not make every later allegation true. It makes the paradoxical sentence hold for this file. The record of the refusal is a record. Publishing it does not require the department to agree with him. It requires the department’s own sentence to remain available to be read.

Number fourteen. A public wrong, and the correction that has not been spoken

At about one hour the video says a wrong done in public cannot be repaired by a whisper in an alley. The mouths that mocked must say, in the open, that they were wrong. If they will not, what they built on the lie comes down. The close is that what was stolen is being restored, what was mocked is being honoured, and the story is closing in glory. “Your honor is unstoppable.”

No office, in the letters opened, has said in public that it was wrong about him. The bank said it was wrong about one act: the removal of NetBank on 15 October 2026, which it called a staff error, and for which it offered $500. That is a narrow admission about access. It is not an admission about the ABN complaints, the transfers, or any wider harm. The information decision was varied so that five documents were released. A variation is not an apology, and it is not a statement that the first refusal was an attempt to erase a man. The Attorney-General’s Department apologised for delay. It did not apologise for a decision on the facts, because it did not make one.

The video wants a crowd to hear “we were wrong.” The record, so far as it was opened, contains no such sentence from a minister, a police force, an ombudsman, or a disability agency. The public thing that does exist is this publication and the letters it links. That is a public record. It is not the public correction the video demands. The difference should be left visible. A witness can put the letters in the square. He cannot, by putting them there, force the authors of the letters to recant.

What can be asked, in the open, without pretending the recantation has happened, is the list the SOS already writes. Food. A safe room for him and for Crystal. A working telephone. A named person. An independent look at the complaints. Those are not glory. They are the correction that would be visible in a life, rather than in a metaphor.

What the fourteen numbers come to, and only from the pages read

The video is one movement. Silence, then a ruined name, then mockery of a decision, then innocence as a mirror, then a camera, then a reversed examination, then a scapegoat, then a cashed reputation, then a scale, then a future self, then restraint, then a delay, then silence as the exhibit, then a name rewritten, then a demand that the correction be as public as the harm.

That story does not require the universe to be furious. It requires the offices to answer what they wrote. It does not require his innocence to be declared by this page. It requires the allegations to stay allegations until the records he asked for are produced or refused in writing. It does not require revenge. The command’s definition holds. Accountability without revenge. Truth without fabrication. Justice without prejudice. Humanity without exception.
